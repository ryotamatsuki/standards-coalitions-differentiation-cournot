## Attempted formal absorption and the surviving economic object

The abstract reduced-form object is `Wtilde_i(H,g)=W_i(H,g,x*(H,g))`. Backward induction after substituting a private Nash equilibrium is standard. The local decomposition `dWtilde/dg=W_g+W_x x_g`, implicit response `x_g=-J^{-1}F_g`, and three-country edge accounting `(i-o)'=2m'` are calculus/symmetry tools, not independent economic discoveries.

The potentially informative conjunction is instead:

1. Starting from the same SW Nash design, the same government menu and consent rules admit a beneficial integrated proposal if designs are frozen.
2. Private residual redesign changes **national** incidence enough that `m_N'(0)<0` and `(i_N-o_N)'(0)<0`, even though `i_N'(0)>0`.
3. If the all-depth proof is correct, another depth cannot rescue the inherited-depth route, while a direct-grand proposal can still improve every country's welfare.

An absorber must preserve the relevant players' strategy sets, their national objectives, continuation choices, rejection alternatives and equilibrium concept sufficiently to deliver that conjunction as a direct special case or easy corollary. None of the directly inspected propositions supplies that mapping. Combining a known design-policy insight with a known outside-option coalition insight **does** make the result economically anticipated, but does not logically derive its strict signs or all-depth government outcome. Absence of a direct mapping also does not itself establish sufficient importance.

## Economic judgment, beyond formula novelty

The manuscript has a defensible **limited explanatory contribution**: standardizing one dimension can weaken incentives to maintain costly differentiation on an unregulated dimension; the resulting Nash response falls on domestic consumers and on the home firm's export earnings, so a fixed-product policy appraisal can predict the wrong participation decision. The important result is not that private design is socially imperfect. It is that this error can overturn the attainable agreement under a held-fixed institutional procedure while leaving direct multilateral gains positive.

The model must explain that interpretation more concretely. Here `x_i` enters cross-product substitution, while `K_i=κ(1-x_i)^2/2` makes lower residual substitutability costly. Thus raising `x_i` economizes on maintaining differentiation; it is not an investment that monotonically raises intrinsic quality. At the stated conditions, `ρ>η` makes the government edge alter the net competitive interaction and induces higher `x`. Saying only “a higher level of the model's residual attribute” obscures this key economic tradeoff. A short interpretation grounded in the equations is needed; a real-world empirical claim or additional model is not required.

The frozen-design benchmark is a controlled decomposition, not a proposed implementable regulation. It changes firms' feasible design choices while preserving the government's game; it therefore identifies the role of adjustment, but does not alone establish the welfare case for freezing products or for a costly redesign tax. Similarly, a unique local `λ_c` can illuminate the mechanism without providing a global SW-to-IS policy threshold.

**Value verdict:** The currently demonstrated mechanism is more than a new coefficient in a known formula, provided the government equilibrium and economic demand foundation withstand the separate proof audit. It is a plausible contribution to a specialized theory paper, with moderate rather than field-changing importance. Its publication value is **CONDITIONAL**, not established simply by an open neighborhood or a rigorous computation. If the all-depth government contrast falls, retaining only the chain-rule decomposition and an uncalibrated sign example would leave a substantially weaker, possibly note-sized contribution. No extension to unrestricted bargaining is required as a condition of this verdict; accurately stated protocol specificity is sufficient.

## Referee 2 report

### Summary

The paper nests a private residual-design Nash game inside a three-government compatibility proposal game. Its promising result is a controlled reversal in national consent and, subject to verification, the globally preferred terminal regime. Existing literature substantially anticipates the components; the defensible increment is their particular national-incidence consequence under a specified accession procedure.

### Major concerns

1. **Economic interpretation of the private choice.** The manuscript names a residual attribute but should say what the cost and cross-substitution specification imply: differentiation is costly and redesign economizes on it. Otherwise an apparently novel “design response” remains an opaque parameterization.
2. **Published predecessor precision.** Use final GMS Props. 1–5 and pp. 238–239 rather than the outdated Stage 24 access status. Explain both its overlapping design-policy channel and the distinct national accession comparison. Relegating this to a vague disclaimer is inadequate.
3. **Contribution rests on a conjunction.** The local identity and symmetry lemma cannot carry a full-paper claim alone. The all-depth equilibrium result must match the actual model and proof; an open neighborhood around one witness establishes non-knife-edge existence, not large quantitative importance or portability across institutions.
4. **Residual closest-source limits.** BFG's horizontal supplement and CH's main theorems remain uninspected; Klimenko's final theorem scope is unresolved. Keep a bounded unresolved-priority ledger. Do not state that these sources have been fully ruled out.
5. **Institution as condition.** The useful diagnostic is that a direct grand proposal works while inherited-depth accession does not. This makes an implemented accession constraint economically relevant only where the stated inherited-specification process is a plausible conditional institution. It cannot support a general claim that product redesign prevents international cooperation.

### Minor concerns

- Replace self-referential phrases about what an editor might reasonably ask with a direct explanation of the result's economic content.
- State that common national welfare accounting is inherited from prior trade/standards work; it is the incidence comparison that is new here.
- Keep C1 as a short mechanism boundary. Move most C2 derivation to an appendix because the design-inactive face does not demonstrate the headline channel.
- Avoid describing a private firm's veto, a sovereign consent constraint and an endogenous protocol as interchangeable.

### Potentially fatal objections

No directly inspected prior proposition has been shown to absorb the exact central result. A fatal novelty verdict would require an actual theorem/game/payoff mapping, not a claim that “externalities are well known.” Conversely, if the mathematical audit invalidates the global government contrast or the demand system supporting national welfare, the surviving economic increment may be too weak for the advertised full paper. Full-main access limitations do not permit resolving that concern in the paper's favor.

### Recommended decision

**MAJOR REVISION / CONDITIONAL GO on the contribution-and-prior-art axis only.** Structural novelty: CONDITIONAL. Mechanism novelty: CONDITIONAL (specific incidence conjunction, not generic design-policy interaction). Economic significance: CONDITIONAL but credible at specialized-journal scale if the equilibrium and welfare foundation survive. No direct absorption finding; no exhaustive-priority certification. This recommendation does not override a FAIL from the independent mathematical audit.

## Editor-facing contribution statement (129 words)

International compatibility agreements are evaluated using products that firms may subsequently redesign. This paper asks whether that private adjustment can reverse national consent without changing governments' bargaining opportunities. In a three-country model, residual-design Nash responses affect domestic consumer surplus and the resident firm's worldwide profits, even after the firm's own optimality condition is imposed. These effects can overturn a positive fixed-design participation gain and, under the specified inherited-depth accession procedure, change the preferred agreement from multilateral integration to fragmentation. A direct multilateral offer remains beneficial, which identifies the institutional limit of the result. The contribution is therefore a controlled explanation of how private design and national participation interact, rather than a new claim that standards coalitions can fail. Its force depends on the validity of the global equilibrium comparison.
