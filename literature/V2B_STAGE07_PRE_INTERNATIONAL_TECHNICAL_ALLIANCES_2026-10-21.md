# Stage 7 Institutional Audit — Bilateral Technical Cooperation Before Global Standardization

**Date / evidence cutoff:** 2026-10-21. **Role:** V2b institutionally credible motivating examples, *not* evidence that the model's predicted SW outcome actually occurred in a named historical case. **No changes to the canonical V2b model, mathematical certificates, or Stage-6 restricted novelty verdict.** Sources prioritized: originating standards associations, government/standardization bodies.

## Research question

Can V2b (two governments/standards sponsors coordinate technical interface at compatibility level g, a third may participate or keep its technology, and firms develop differentiated products after commitments) be interpreted as **pre-ISO/IEC/ITU regional or bilateral technical development**, rather than as ISO's global committee voting? **YES, with important constraints**.

### Case A — Japan–China EV DC charging: ChaoJi, strongest engineering match

- **Aug 28, 2018:** Japan-based **CHAdeMO Association**, and China's **China Electricity Council** (CEC, a standards actor approved by Chinese government) signed MoU for joint development/harmonization of a next-gen ultra-high power charging interface to reconcile CHAdeMO and GB/T.
- **July 19, 2019:** first international ChaoJi meeting in Tokyo with IEC experts and expanded members; 60 professionals from Europe and Asia participated; technical working groups for connectors/adapters, safety, communication/backward compatibility. This is **participation beyond original A/B**, not verified as *sovereign formal accession*.
- **April 2020:** technical paper for CHAdeMO 3.0 using the common ChaoJi plug; Chinese GB/T communication protocol was separately maintained initially. The physical plug compatibility **does not imply identical final systems**; this strongly motivates separation `g` (interface) from `x_i` (residual design).
- **2023:** ChaoJi-related Chinese national GB/T 18487.1, GB/T 27930 and GB/T 20234.4 standards announced; IEC proposals/discussion ongoing.
- **2022–26:** CHAdeMO official technical history links Ultra-ChaoJi standards development to IEC 61851-23-3 and IEC TS 63379; IEC lists 63379 as technical specification Jan 2026 and 61851-23-3 as standard Aug 2026. **Do not claim** IEC 63379 is itself identical to the *original* 2020 Japanese/Chinese ChaoJi protocol: megawatt charger subtrack and distinct configurations/standards work exist.
- Sources:
  - CHAdeMO organization history: https://www.chademo.com/about-us/organisation
  - Original 2018–20 project chronology: https://www.chademo.com/ja/next-generation-charging-demo-in-kashima
  - 2020 common plug with separate GB/T/CHAdeMO communications: https://www.chademo.com/ja/chademo3-0
  - 2023 GB/T approvals: https://www.chademo.com/ja/chaoji_gbt
  - Official CHAdeMO IEC work plan: https://www.chademo.com/ja/technology/high-power
  - METI natural-language institutional recognition of the 2018 bilateral international-standardization step: https://www.enecho.meti.go.jp/about/special/johoteikyo/evs31.html
  - IEC separate publications: https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/67975 and https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/69016
- **Fit:** initial two stakeholders, actual technical g, more experts join, later national+international standards, design freedom in other functions. **Nonfit:** private/government-approved standards associations rather than two governments exercising national welfare; third-country experts join working groups, not known C sovereign application and AB unanimous veto; charger/EV installed base is two-sided/network stock, not V2b's same-market fulfilled contemporaneous quantity; other EV products remain substitutes only if properly scoped as competing charger makers/vehicle makers.

### Case B — Franco-German GSM 1984 → thirteen-country memorandum 1987; strongest government-cooperation match

- **1982:** pan-European GSM initiative already established under CEPT. **A/B did not create the entire project ex nihilo**.
- **1984:** French and German administrations jointly agreed GSM development.
- **1987:** 15 operators from 13 countries signed MoU committing to GSM deployment; 1989 GSM became ETSI technical committee domain. Firms could differentiate handsets and carriers while preserving the mobile air interface, network roaming/interoperability.
- Source: original GSMA timeline https://www.gsma.com/about-us/who-we-are/our-history/ ; GSMA anniversary statement with original signatories https://www.gsma.com/newsroom/press-release/global-mobile-communication-is-20-years-old/ ; ETSI history https://www.etsi.org/about/history/.
- **Fit:** two-country governments cooperate and wider later agreement, interoperability plus residual commercial variety. **Nonfit:** multinational standards setting was already underway; no verified later third-party veto; GSM delivered successful wide coordination, not V2b's blocked agreement. Later firms' endogenous product similarity as causal response to governments' commitment has not been established empirically.

### Case C — US–EU Trade and Technology Council (TTC), bilateral* governmental coordination before international forums

- 2021 US and EU TTC established technology standards working group; 2022 Strategic Standardisation Information mechanism shares international standards positions; 2023 joint statements support 3D additive manufacturing ISO/CEN/ASTM coordination and outreach to third countries, and global standard-setting work.
- Source: USTR 2021 joint statement https://ustr.gov/about-us/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2021/september/us-eu-trade-and-technology-council-inaugural-joint-statement ; EC historical TTC https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/factpages/eu-us-trade-and-technology-council-2021-2024 ; US Commerce 2023 cooperation https://www.commerce.gov/news/press-releases/2023/05/us-eu-joint-statement-trade-and-technology-council .
- **Fit:** explicitly governmental early-stage standards alignment and third-country internationalization. **Nonfit:** EU is a multistate polity and standards bodies, not single state B; information-sharing and voting alignment are not a deployed interface depth g>0; no C accession/veto. V2b SU real cross-compatibility cannot be interpreted as merely exchanging technical opinions.

### Case D — Australia–New Zealand joint AS/NZS standards, genuine bilateral standard production but weak global-sequencing match

- Two national standards organizations operate joint committees and produce thousands of AS/NZS standards, used in law/industry; Standards NZ explicitly says **adopt international standards FIRST where practicable**, create joint standards otherwise.
- Source: https://www.standards.govt.nz/about/working-with-international-partners
- **Fit:** stable country-level bilateral technical agreement coexisting with differentiated commercial products; **Nonfit:** no documented C later admission or conversion of a specific joint standard to ISO. It is a counterexample to falsely assuming all national collaboration is pre-ISO; sometimes international standard comes first.

## Reviewer-style decision / contribution
- **Institutional plausibility:** POSITIVE for *pre-international bilateral technical cooperation/consortia that later invite broader parties*; strongest for **2018 Japan–China ChaoJi** (engineering) and **1984 France–Germany GSM** (country-government coalition history).
- **Critical caveat:** V2b models **a binding technical compatibility choice that affects goods' substitutability/network utility at Stage 1**. Mere nonbinding pre-standard research MoU does NOT imply positive g or immediate consumer effects, so it cannot rescue an unrealistic government SU assumption without modifying primitives.
- **Critical caveat:** none of these examples validates the model's exact discriminatory entrant consent protocol nor empirically proves firms' post-agreement residual-design reaction drives a three-country accession veto. Classify as credible motivating cases only, not tests or demonstrations of the central theorem.
- **Practical framing:** "regional/bilateral *pre-international technical standards alliance with later third-party participation*" rather than "ISO's internal 2+1 voting procedure" or "USB-C mandatory adoption". Identify government or standards-body delegation explicitly. This change is **framing and evidence**, not a silent alteration of actor role or formal timing.
- **Stage 7 routing:** examples strengthen institutional plausibility of initial cooperation, but exact C-veto and particular simultaneous game remains stylized; **no additional mathematical GO or claim of cross-function-form generality** results from this evidence. Stage 7.5 editorial significance and Stage 7.5A institutional red-team still required.

**Historical studies do not demonstrate a real-world negative coalition outcome caused by endogenous design.** Do not claim direct fit to a known failure.
