**Audit date and source cutoff:** 2026-10-21. **Purpose:** compare the exact Stage-4A-certified restricted V2b proposition to *actual* theorems rather than setup buzzwords. This document supersedes no earlier model math or source ledger; it updates Stage-2 priority estimates.

## Canonical research claim being attacked
Three sovereigns A,B,C: A proposes AB standard compatibility depth g, B accepts/rejects; C may seek admission, A/B unanimously admit at **unchanged** g; *only after policy* each domestic firm chooses a privately costly continuous residual product homogeneity x_i; firms then compete in every country's Cournot market. Each government maximizes its domestic consumer surplus plus world profits of its home firm less its once-paid design cost. Cross-substitution B_ij=rho H_ij+(1-rho)(x_i+x_j)/2; network benefit eta H. At (a-c,eta,rho,kappa)=(1,.758,.9,.8505), the endogenous continuation implies no positive-depth coalition for any g, whereas freezing each x_i at its SW Nash design while keeping upstream game and cost levels identical makes a positive-depth IS preferable to every terminal SU and SW policy. Stage4A claims this on an unquantified open neighborhood, not a general-function theorem.

## Whole-game reduction attempt

Application-neutral mathematical object:
- 3 principals with national-welfare objective choose one coalition graph H(S,g) through a fixed sequential consent rule, no g revision on accession;
- given H, three agents choose continuous costly x via a concave Nash game;
- given (H,x), an LQ Cournot game pins q and national social surpluses;
- induced reduced-form government partition payoff \widetilde W_i(S,g)=W_i(S,g,x^*(S,g));
- frozen x benchmark replaces x^*(S,g) with x^*(SW,0) **without changing governments' policy space or welfare cost level**.

The reduced-form government game is an **instance of generalized extensive-form coalition formation with externalities**, which absorbs algorithmic backward-induction. However, reductions to Ray–Vohra or Economides–Skrzypacz do not preserve both sovereign (not firm) welfare payoffs AND the inherited continuous g AND post-policy private design best response as a special case. Cabral–Salant's innovation-based nonstandardization theorem overlaps strongly with the mechanism but has different choice order, payoff beneficiary and industrial object. Buchholz–Hattori overlaps strategic technology-coalition interaction but places technology before coalition decisions and has no standard-compatibility competition matrix.

**Critical distinction:** absence of an exact full game in accessible papers does NOT by itself certify a new theorem, because generic comparative-statics/coalition formation may still absorb the conditional policy reversal. Exhaustively identifying a generic parent is unresolved at this audit cutoff.

## Claim statuses after re-kill

- **KILLED as novel:** standards enhance network benefits but strengthen competition; standardized firms differentiate strategically; endogenous private innovation can make standards fragmentation preferable to static standardization (Cabral–Salant); future coalition structure influences endogenous technologies (Buchholz–Hattori); C veto and regional/multilateral standards; concave Nash uniqueness; derivative/graph-symmetry lemma.
- **PARTIALLY ABSORBED, STILL UNDER AUDIT:** exact post-government privately chosen substitution margin flips both A/B SU formation and C IS accession gains in a single 3-country fixed-depth extensive-form game, leading to frozen-IS vs endogenous-no-agreement regime reversal for an open set around a rational parameter witness. 
- **NOT YET EVIDENCE-SCOPED NOVELTY CERTIFIED** due to Cabral–Salant/coalition parent theoretical scope and unavailable decisive Gandal–Shy full text.
- **Strongest question to falsify:** is this no more than the Cabral–Salant R&D-standardization deterrence logic restated with another downstream investment variable and an elementary three-government partition payoff, with zero independent welfare/policy insight?
