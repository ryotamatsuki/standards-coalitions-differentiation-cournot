Endogenous Product Design and Participation in International Compatibility Agreements

## Abstract: REWRITE

**Insertion/selection decision:** Replace the whole abstract; no new result is introduced.

Can firms’ product redesign change whether governments agree on compatibility standards? We study three countries whose firms choose costly residual differentiation before competing in quantities. Governments negotiate compatibility through a bilateral-first procedure in which subsequent accession inherits the original depth. We compare this game with a counterfactual that fixes product designs at their no-agreement Nash levels. Private redesign affects domestic consumer surplus and the home firm’s worldwide profits; these national effects are not eliminated by the firm’s own-profit first-order condition. Under explicit sufficient conditions, redesign reverses both a bilateral member’s stand-alone welfare gain and the outsider’s gain from accession. A computer-assisted proof establishes an open parameter neighborhood in which the government-optimal outcome switches from integration with fixed designs to fragmentation with endogenous designs. Yet a direct multilateral proposal can benefit all three countries. The result identifies how private design responses and inherited agreement terms jointly determine national consent, rather than a general obstacle to compatibility cooperation.

## Introduction opening: REWRITE

**Insertion/selection decision:** Replace the opening and integrate the precise predecessor paragraph after these paragraphs. Avoid adding real-world examples unsupported by the current model.

Compatibility agreements coordinate a common interface, while leaving firms free to choose the remaining features of their products. Governments therefore need to evaluate the products that firms will supply after an agreement, rather than only the products available before it. This distinction matters because a government values both domestic consumer surplus and the worldwide profits of its resident firm, whereas a firm chooses its design to maximize its own profit.

We ask whether this difference in objectives can reverse governments’ consent to an agreement without changing the government’s negotiation procedure. Our model separates publicly negotiated compatibility depth from privately chosen residual differentiation. Holding the economic primitives and government procedure fixed, we compare endogenous firm design with a counterfactual that constrains designs to their no-agreement Nash levels. The comparison isolates the national welfare incidence of allowing firms to redesign their products.

The main result is a change in the implemented government outcome. In an open neighborhood of the benchmark parameters, the fixed-design counterfactual supports a government-optimal integrated agreement, whereas endogenous design produces fragmentation at every positive depth under the bilateral-first accession procedure. This failure coexists with a direct integrated proposal that improves every country’s welfare. The distinction between these routes is central: accession is evaluated against the bilateral arrangement, whereas a direct grand proposal is evaluated against fragmentation.

## Central contribution

**Insertion/selection decision:** Use once in the Introduction, with the national-incidence formula if desired.

The contribution is a controlled comparison of national consent with and without downstream residual redesign. Firms’ own-profit optimality leaves two national effects: the response of domestic consumer surplus and the response of the home firm’s profits to its rivals’ designs. We derive these effects and show that they can overturn the direct benefit of compatibility. Three-country symmetry links the member’s stand-alone welfare derivative to the outsider’s accession derivative. ## Model interpretation

**Insertion/selection decision:** Insert after eq.(2).

Each firm chooses residual similarity x_i; a higher x_i saves the cost of differentiation. The government chooses the depth g of compatibility between goods supplied from member countries. A deeper agreement has two effects in demand: it increases cross-product substitution through B and expands the network interaction through H. The parameter rho weights the public component of substitution, while eta measures the network benefit. Hence greater compatibility need not raise every country’s welfare even before firms adjust their designs. Network quantities in the model are measured within each destination market; the same origin-based compatibility links apply in all three markets.

## Frozen-design counterfactual

**Insertion/selection decision:** Replace §4.2 opening; design costs must remain evaluated at z.

In the fixed-design counterfactual, firms are constrained to x_i=z at every policy history, where z is their no-agreement Nash design under the same primitives. The design cost remains K_i(z), and firms continue to play the Cournot quantity game in each destination market. This is a constraint used to isolate the effect of redesign; it is not an assertion that x_i=z remains a design Nash equilibrium after policy changes.

## Mechanism

**Insertion/selection decision:** Replace the economic interpretation after Lemma 2.

Near fragmentation at the benchmark, a deeper bilateral agreement reduces the member firms’ private return to maintaining costly residual differentiation. Their equilibrium similarity choices rise, and the outsider also adjusts its design. Each firm accounts for its own design cost and operating profit, but not for the resulting loss of domestic consumer surplus. Moreover, a home firm’s first-order condition does not remove the effects of its rivals’ design changes on its worldwide profits. Together these responses subtract from the member’s fixed-design welfare gain. When this induced loss exceeds the direct gain, both the stand-alone bilateral margin and, by three-country symmetry, the outsider’s accession margin become negative.

## Government backward induction

**Insertion/selection decision:** Use once immediately after the main theorem or after Table 2; not both.

The rejection sequence is essential. At an enlargement history, the outsider prefers the bilateral outside option to integration and therefore rejects accession. Anticipating this rejection, the prospective initial member compares the bilateral payoff with fragmentation. Because that comparison is also negative, the initial bilateral proposal is rejected. A direct integrated proposal instead gives the outsider fragmentation as its rejection payoff; it can therefore be accepted even though enlargement of the bilateral arrangement is rejected.

## All-depth proof architecture

**Insertion/selection decision:** Keep the displayed theorem and exact appendix details. This paragraph organizes, rather than replaces, its logical proof.

The proof has three steps. First, Appendix D establishes positive quantities and a unique design Nash equilibrium on the full strategy cube; symmetry then permits the reduced bilateral and integrated systems. Second, interval inclusions cover every depth in [0,1] and bound the endogenous derivatives of m and i-o strictly below zero. Since both welfare gaps vanish at zero depth, integration makes the corresponding level gaps negative at every positive depth. In the fixed-design counterfactual, a strictly accepted integrated proposal at g=1/20 dominates fragmentation and an upper bound on the bilateral payoff at every depth. Maximizing integrated welfare over the closed accepted-depth set therefore implements integration. Third, the uniform regularity and strict welfare margins persist in a sufficiently small common neighborhood of the primitives. This gives the open-set result without assigning a numerical radius to that neighborhood.

## Closest predecessor

## Near-face coefficient

### Optional explicit inward-continuation bridge for Appendix F

The following supplies the specific detail requested in 13R-09 without a new model. Let beta=1-rho, alpha=2-eta, and w=(1-eta)g on the face. Under bilateral compatibility at beta=0, write q_a^0=d/(alpha+w) and q_c^0=d/alpha. Direct inversion gives
\[
(q_{x_A})_A=-\beta a_A(g)+O(\beta^2),\quad
 a_A(g)=\frac{(\alpha-w)q_a^0+\alpha q_c^0}{2(\alpha^2-w^2)}>0,
\]
\[
(q_{x_C})_C=-\beta a_C(g)+O(\beta^2),\quad a_C(g)=q_a^0/\alpha>0.
\]
For integrated compatibility,
\[
a_i(g)=\frac{\alpha d}{(\alpha-w)(\alpha+2w)^2}>0.
\]
The unilateral FOC therefore implies
\[
1-x_i=\frac{6\beta q_i^0 a_i(g)}{\kappa}+O(\beta^2)>0.
\]
On a compact eta interval inside (0,1), the leading coefficients are uniformly positive for g in [0,1]. The smooth unconstrained root consequently enters the design cube for sufficiently small beta>0. At beta=0 the optimum remains x_i=1; it must not be described as interior on that face.
