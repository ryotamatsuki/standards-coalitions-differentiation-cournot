## Risk comparison: orthogonal editorial asks, not one prestige ladder
- **JPET:** *why does a government nationally reject and what is economically new beyond Takarada 2020?* Supported by frozen-vs-Nash controlled decomposition and universal-in-g (at center) absence plus direct-grand counterfactual. Requires crisp Introduction distinction.
- **JoE:** *why isn't this BFG/Cheng quality/veto in another costume?* Answer only with actual different sovereign objectives/home export profits/three-government inherited-depth participation and controlled same-game frozen design, not general 'our model has more countries'.
- **JITE:** *what economic institutional rule matters and why is it coherent?* Emphasize specified accession to an already-selected technical agreement, not universal international standards organizations; openly state non-endogenized negotiating costs and renegotiation exclusions.
- **RIO:** *what does the firm market do that a coalition game without Cournot would not?* Need trace CS-home-world-profit incidence and actual private Nash residual design. No fabricated measured magnitudes or industry examples.
- **BEJTE:** *is there a general theorem of game theory?* No; claim a distinctive model-specific equilibrium mechanism with all-depth certificate and exactly restricted institution. Present original exact theorem, not just `D-L` formula.
- **RNE:** *what does this add to network economics?* Model explicitly combines network consumption η, technical weighting ρ, firms residual similarity x, and national consent. No extrapolation to universal telecom/WTO.
- **JICT:** *what can policymakers test or implement?* This model diagnoses why fixed-design evaluation mispredicts consent and why inherited vs direct offers differ. It does **not** derive a feasible bargaining mechanism, transfers, or empirical policy effect.
