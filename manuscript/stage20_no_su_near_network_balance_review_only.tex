\subsection{Can bilateral standards unions be government-optimal?}

Let $s$ denote national welfare in fragmentation,
$m(g)$ and $o(g)$ the member and outsider national welfare
under a bilateral standards union, and $i(g)$ the common
national welfare under a fully participating standards union.
Every payoff is computed after the firms' true design-Nash
and fulfilled-expectations Cournot continuation. The
bilateral-first inherited-depth game has an internationally
accepted outcome only when
\[
 i(g)\geq\max\{s,m(g),o(g)\}.
\]
A terminal bilateral union is feasible when
\[
 m(g)\geq s,\quad
 \bigl(i(g)<m(g)\ \text{or}\ i(g)<o(g)\bigr).
\]
In particular, $m(g)>s$ does not establish a \emph{government-
optimal} bilateral union; the proposer may choose another
depth that induces profitable multilateral accession.

\begin{proposition}[Global multilateral dominance around balanced
network and substitution effects]
Fix an admissible parameter configuration satisfying
$\rho_0=\eta_0\in(0,1)$, $d>0$, $\kappa>0$, $\lambda\geq0$.
Suppose the post-policy full three-firm design Nash is
symmetric, uniquely interior, and regular, and equilibrium
quantities are strictly positive with strict uniform margins
over the full compact policy domain. Then the government's
unique optimal depth and final coalition are
\[
 g^*=1,\qquad {\rm regime}={\rm IS}.
\]
Moreover, there exists an open neighborhood of the
configuration in primitive parameter space in which the
unique optimal government policy remains $g^*=1$ and
the final coalition remains IS; SU and SW are never optimal
there (under weak acceptance at exact indifference).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The realized Cournot matrix is
$M=I+(\rho-\eta)H+(1-\rho)X$.
At $\eta=\rho$ it is independent of $H$, and consequently
of the government's coalition and depth for any fixed
firm-design profile. The private firm's worldwide profit and
design cost are therefore independent of the government's
history. Uniqueness implies all histories have the same
symmetric equilibrium product design $\bar x$ and quantities
\[
q_0=\frac{d}{2-\eta+2(1-\rho)\bar x}>0.
\]
Because consumer surplus is $q^\top Bq/2$, introducing
one compatibility edge of depth $g$ adds precisely
$\rho g q_0^2$ to consumer surplus per country while
all firm incomes remain unchanged. Therefore
\[
W_i^{SW}=s,\qquad W_i^{SU}(g)=s+\rho gq_0^2,\qquad
W_i^{IS}(g)=s+3\rho gq_0^2.
\]
These formulae hold for ALL countries, including the
SU outsider. Grand accession at $g=1$ is strictly
acceptable because $i(1)-m(1)=i(1)-o(1)
=2\rho q_0^2>0$, and $i(1)-s=3\rho q_0^2>0$.
Also
\[
i(1)-\sup_{0\le g\le1}m(g)
=2\rho q_0^2>0,\qquad
\frac{di(g)}{dg}=3\rho q_0^2>0.
\]
Hence accepted IS at full depth strictly dominates
every conceivable terminal SU payoff and fragmentation,
and is uniquely optimal among IS offers.
Under the stated strict regularity, the firms' Nash
continuations and national payoffs vary continuously
with the primitives and jointly continuously
differentiably with depth uniformly over the compact
policy domain. Each of the displayed strict margins
and the strictly positive $\inf_{g\in[0,1]}i_g$
persists in some common neighborhood.
The same globally optimal depth and regime follow
throughout that neighborhood.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Nonresult to disclose.}
The theorem does not exclude SU in ALL other admissible
parameter configurations. A deterministic floating-point
scan of 300{,}000 configurations (140 depths each, seed 21)
reported 208{,}606 configurations with valid numerical
roots at all 140 tested depths, and **no case** in which
terminal SU exceeded both SW and the accepted IS
proposal envelope. This is numerical counterexample
search, NOT a proof of global nonexistence.
Some apparent bilateral winners in the high-network
region use invalid negative-output continuations at
other depths, and must not be claimed as equilibria.
A full three-region SW--SU--IS theorem is unproved.


## Correction (2026-10-22): strict interior numerical search gate
The FIRST run mistakenly treated a symmetric IS firm-design output y=0 as passing an interior strategy check. A fresh full 300000-draw seeded rerun, changing the admissibility condition from y>=0 to **0<y<1** and requiring 0<u,v<1 on every sampled depth, yields **190388** fully 140-point grid-valid draws, **3429** draws with some SU-feasible offer, and **zero** fully grid-valid SU-optimal winners. Prior counts 208606 and 3696 are superseded. Numerical Newton and FOC checks cannot establish full all-g Nash optimality; the stress search is explicitly NOT proof.
