\subsection{A prospective network-dependent standards-regime classification}

Let $s$ denote fragmentation welfare per country and define $m(g)$,
$o(g)$ and $i(g)$ as the bilateral-member, bilateral-outsider and grand
national welfare, respectively, AFTER the post-policy design Nash is solved.
Under the bilateral-first/inherited-depth protocol, a grand terminal
agreement at $g$ is feasible when
\[
 i(g)\ge s,\qquad i(g)\ge o(g),\qquad i(g)\ge m(g).
\]
It is incorrect to add $m(g)\ge s$ as an independent requirement if the
second bilateral country anticipates successful grand accession.
A terminal bilateral outcome instead requires $m(g)\ge s$ and an
enlargement rejection, $i(g)<o(g)$ or $i(g)<m(g)$, under weak-vote
acceptance. These inequalities define the regimes' feasible depth sets.
The government optimizes the appropriate continuation payoff over those
sets; feasibility is not the same as proposer optimality.

\begin{proposition}[Network--substitution balance and full harmonization]
Suppose $\rho=\eta\in(0,1)$, $d>0$ and the unchanged post-policy private
design game has an interior, unique symmetric Nash equilibrium $z$.
Then the private design and output are invariant to any policy depth and
to the choice between fragmented, bilateral and grand standardization.
Let $q_0=d/(2-\eta+2(1-\rho)z)>0$ and $s=W_i^{SW}$.
For all countries and depths,
\[
 W_i^{SU}(g)=s+\rho gq_0^2,\qquad
 W_i^{IS}(g)=s+3\rho gq_0^2.
\]
Therefore the government's unique substantive optimal outcome is full
grand harmonization, $IS$ at $g^*=1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
With $\eta=\rho$, the off-diagonal technical link has zero coefficient
in the fulfilled-expectations Cournot matrix $M=I+B-\eta H$;
the entire private design/quantity subgame is therefore policy-invariant.
In consumer surplus $q^\top Bq/2$, each undirected policy link contributes
$\rho g q_0^2$ while quantities and domestic firm's optimized income
remain unchanged. SU installs one link and IS three. Every bilateral,
outsider accession and incumbent vote is strictly positive and the
proposer's payoff strictly increases with $g$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The open three-region question.}
The exact threshold ordering $SW\to SU\to IS$ along a primitive
parameter path is NOT yet established. The above proposition proves only
one structural boundary outcome; the inherited baseline also proves SW
at $\eta=0.758,\rho=0.9,\lambda=0$. Coarse numerical scans have not
identified an optimal terminal SU along that network-intensity path.
A genuine three-region main theorem requires proving nonempty SU
optimality and strict global envelope crossings or else proving why
SU cannot be optimal. Do not extrapolate the sign of the private-design
response $y_g$ into the sign of the optimal government regime.
