## 2. Proposition-level reading

### Gandal–Shy: the national-welfare ownership structure and coalitions are established

The primitives use fixed, equally spaced Salop brands; governments choose recognition before firms choose prices. Section 2.6, Eq. (11), aggregates domestic consumer surplus and the home firm's worldwide profits. Props 1 and 4 give dominant recognition in the restricted binary-policy benchmark. Props 2–3 identify conversion-cost ranges favoring exclusionary bilateral unions; Prop 5 favors worldwide recognition in the network-benefit case. Their proofs use explicit country-welfare tables and price/share formulas, including foreclosure cases.

The paper does not contain a post-policy residual-design Nash problem. Its recognition benchmark must not be relabelled V2b fragmentation: the former already permits worldwide recognition. Thus it does not directly supply V2b's comparison between fixed and reoptimized designs within the same inherited-depth agenda. The exact V2b result is not a variable renaming of those five propositions; national profit incidence and coalition comparisons themselves are known.

### Petropoulou: agreement inefficiency is known, but the outside-option distinction matters

The published abstract confirms endogenous national minimum standards, quality-dependent variable costs and segmented markets. DP858 studies government standards followed by firms' quality and price choices. Its Props 7–8 identify reciprocal adjustments from symmetric equilibria; Prop 9 reaches the world optimum from symmetric starting points. In the asymmetric case, Prop 10 and Lemma 11 show restricted cooperation: the world optimum is not Pareto-improving from the asymmetric disagreement point without transfers. These statements are WP statements.

This is important overlap for “standards plus endogenous product characteristics plus national welfare.” However, V2b's claimed inaccessible agreement is Pareto-improving relative to fragmentation, while the accession decision compares that agreement with outsider welfare after an existing bilateral union. The distinction concerns continuation payoffs and a fixed-versus-endogenous-design comparison, not simply the number of countries. Published proof correspondence remains unresolved.

### Takarada–Kawabata: outsider resistance to multilateralization is directly known

The two-stage model has governments select minimum standards and firms then choose market quantities. Stricter standards reduce consumption externalities but raise compliance costs. Proposition 1 gives cases where regional MR harms its members under separate national welfare maximization. Proposition 2 shows cooperative standard-setting can benefit all countries. Proposition 3(iii) shows regional members gain from multilateral MR while the outsider loses; Appendix C supplies country-welfare differences. Thus the observation that a regional arrangement can obstruct multilateralization through outsider incentives is directly known.

The firms do not choose V2b's independent residual horizontal-design instrument after government standards. The source therefore supplies neither V2b's member–outsider design derivative ranking nor its fixed-design versus endogenous-design reversal theorem. This is a genuine residual question, but absence of that choice in one model is not by itself proof of worldwide novelty. The report does not import the unrelated DOI `10.1007/s10290-025-00607-2`: that DOI concerns countervailing duties, not this DP.

### Buchholz–Hattori: endogenous technology can overturn cooperative gains

In the primary WP, an outsider selects contribution technology, a given group then chooses coalition formation, and public-good provision follows. Under the stated threshold restriction, Prop 1 gives no new-technology adoption in the SPNE. Props 2–3 identify regions where allowing the future coalition reduces total provision or aggregate welfare. The threshold calculations precede the propositions. The paper explicitly separates its coalition-profitability problem from a general membership/withdrawal stability analysis.

Thus “endogenous technology plus coalition formation can reverse a beneficial-cooperation conclusion” is already known. V2b's candidate contribution instead requires proving how a previously chosen technical agreement changes subsequent firms' residual differentiation and country-specific accession margins. Reversing timing alone is not novelty; the distinct equilibrium comparisons must carry the contribution. Published proof-level absorption is unresolved because the 2024 full text was not read.

## 4. What the publication evidence supports

The open RIO article Brander–Spencer (2022) uses quadratic utility and endogenous horizontal differentiation. Its §6 and Props 7–8 compare endogenous-design Bertrand/Cournot outcomes; it explicitly distinguishes analytic variable-profit proof from numerical industry-profit comparison. Appendix 5 uses FOCs, continuity and scaling rather than demanding elementary closed-form investment roots. It demonstrates that a simple functional form and implicit optimal design can support a full article. The relevant standard is an economically informative, properly scoped reversal, not formal generality alone.

JIE's published Gandal–Shy article supports a parallel methodological observation: explicit special-model welfare thresholds and separate market/foreclosure cases can be central results. Neither example establishes a current acceptance criterion, nor makes every new special-case reversal equally valuable. V2b should explain what changes a reader's assessment of feasible agreements after firm redesign, and distinguish local analytic conditions from the existing all-depth certified witness.

## 5. Recommendation to the lead audit

Keep B as a mechanism proposition; present corrected C together with the all-depth institutional counterexample as the main contribution. Use A to explain design incentives and D to isolate the channel's absence. Do not count these as four independent discoveries. The strongest residual claim is that optimizing a nonstandardized product margin changes the *implementable set of agreements* under the same sequential institution, despite a grand agreement improving every country's payoff over fragmentation.

Recommend **CONDITIONAL GO for full-paper development**, with **UNRESOLVED priority clearance** where full published texts remain unavailable. Full-paper suitability should not be rejected simply for Cournot/quadratic primitives; equally, the known coalition and national-welfare components cannot carry novelty by themselves.
