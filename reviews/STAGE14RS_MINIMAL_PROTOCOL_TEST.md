# Why the protocol switch is NOT the selected extension

Original Stage14: for the fixed lambda=0 endogenous-design model at the audited point, a direct grand offer i(g)>s is accepted by all at a sufficiently small g, even while a bilateral-first inherited-depth path fails both bilateral and outsider votes. Therefore merely allowing a grand offer trivially removes the procedural blockage. The bargaining protocol is itself studied in Aghion, Antràs & Helpman (2007, Negotiating Free Trade), Bloch (1996), Ray & Vohra (1999), Caparrós & Péreau (2017), and Münster & Reisinger (2026). A standalone claim that the choice of sequential versus multilateral procedure changes coalition formation is **already known**.

The selected extension keeps the *same protocol* and makes its agreement feasibility differ through only one economically interpretable private adjustment-cost parameter. No claim is made about an endogenous protocol-choice equilibrium or the global efficiency of the inherited-depth rule.
