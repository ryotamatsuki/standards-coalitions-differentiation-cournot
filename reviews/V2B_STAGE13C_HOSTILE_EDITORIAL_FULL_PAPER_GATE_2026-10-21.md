## 3. Strongest skeptical editorial report

> I appreciate the repaired exact calculation, the nonempty domain for the new local mechanism propositions, and the clear separation of technical interface standards from remaining private attributes. The center of the paper is, however, not an entirely new theory of endogenous standardization. Earlier literature includes international firms' endogenous quality and horizontal differentiation under government policy, R&D–standards dynamic feedback, and even a 1998 model in which firms' endogenous technology/quality choices produce different government standards-coordination equilibria. This manuscript adds one careful counterfactual experiment: under exactly the same bilateral-first, inherited-depth government accession procedure, the agreements regarded as feasible under frozen residual product designs can disappear when firms readjust. Conditional grand compatibility still makes every country better off than fragmentation, but it fails at intermediate outside-option votes. This is logically distinct from the inspected earlier results. It may be economically valuable. The referee will nevertheless want to know what is learned beyond a crafted game-tree counterexample and whether the specified agenda reflects a meaningful class of standards negotiations. The newly established conditions B and C give useful insight at the fragmented baseline, but their rigorous 4D region is narrow and they alone are not a global institutional comparative-static theorem. The paper's global result is still one theta0, and the alternative nonlinear fixed-depth accession actually fails at its precommitted depth. In my view a serious full-paper candidate exists, but the abstract and narrative must place the counterfactual mechanism front and center, not oversell generic convergence or globality; the closest citations, including Lambertini and Rossini, must be acknowledged, and inaccessible final texts must remain unresolved before a submission-level priority judgment.

**Recommendation:** Conditional *revise for external referee readiness*; no declaration of a definitive novel universal economic theorem.

## 4. Strongest sympathetic referee report

> The paper tackles an overlooked *decision variable* in international standards economics: not whether total standardization welfare improves and not merely how technology affects firms' profits, but whether national governments can individually consent at every node of a path-dependent admission procedure when firms can adjust all unstandardized attributes after standard selection. The exact same government agenda is evaluated under two private strategy spaces, and the direction of the country-level feasibility result reverses. Existing coalition/standardization papers do not directly provide the three-way combination of proposer full-menu feasibility, post-policy horizontal residual-design Nash, and an internationally Pareto-beneficial yet inaccessible true continuation. The paper now explains the economic mechanism with analytic member–outsider response and country incidence sufficient conditions on a certified open parameter domain, reducing reliance on a lone numerical witness. Its full-policy-domain rational certificate prevents a continuum-vs-grid weakness. In an appropriately delimited theory paper with a clear economic story and complete source scrutiny, this can be a useful incremental yet substantive contribution to IO and government standards coordination.

**Recommendation:** Keep developing full-length article; do not downgrade to note solely because its primitives are quadratic and its institutions stylized.
