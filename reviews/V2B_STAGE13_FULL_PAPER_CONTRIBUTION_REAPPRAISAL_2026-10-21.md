## 2. Unitary mechanism and three economic implications (do not pretend three independent discoveries)

**Single mechanism:** A compatibility commitment changes the private incentives to adjust product attributes outside the formal standard. Those changes affect cross-product competition, domestic consumers, and profits of nationally headquartered firms active in all three markets. The government's problem is the feasibility of a contract, not just the sign of aggregate gains.

### Contribution B: A *Pareto/implementability wedge* is institution-specific, not a welfare paradox about standardization in general

At g=.05 with *true firm design Nash*:
i−s=+0.016934627905 per country, so if the three governments faced an IS offer directly against SW, each strictly gains.
But the AB-first/inherited-g path cannot reach that outcome: at the same g, the outside C's continuation after AB alone satisfies o>i, and AB's terminal member welfare satisfies m<s. National welfare ordering at that point is
o(.05)>i(.05)>s>m(.05)
for the appropriately labeled country-specific continuation payoffs.
This is an OFF-PATH conditional ordering: no positive-depth AB itself forms on equilibrium. It is not a claim that all international compatibility is inefficient; in fact the conditional grand compatibility raises each country's welfare.

**New policy implication candidate:** benefits of a hypothetical international standard alone are insufficient to judge whether staged accession can implement it. Bargaining *agenda and outsider continuation* must be modeled alongside subsequent firm design; a direct grand offer can succeed while the specified incremental path fails.

### Contribution C: Cross-firm design externality changes government welfare, despite a private firm's optimal own design

At an interior private-design Nash, Pi_i,x_i=0, so for any smooth policy path x*(g), writing W_i=CS_i+Pi_i,
dW_i/dg = W_i,g + SUM_j(CS_i,x_j*x'_j) + SUM_{j != i}(Pi_i,x_j*x'_j).
The own-firm profit derivative w.r.t. its OWN optimally chosen design disappears. The **effects of rival firms' redesign and domestic consumer response remain**. This explains how well-optimizing private firms can collectively make an otherwise directly beneficial public standards project unattractive to an individual government without inconsistent private rationality.

At g=0 for member A, the exact theta-specific decomposition is approximately
direct policy: +0.0067679241;
induced domestic consumer term: −0.005216220;
induced rival-design export-profit term: −0.009853524;
total: −0.0083018200.
The net induced adverse effect is about **2.23 times** the positive direct gain. This creates the country-participation sign reversal. **This envelope identity is standard calculus, not a separately novel theorem**; it is a new mechanism application to national coalition voting in the current paper.

## 5. Strongest substantive next theorem under UNCHANGED economic primitives (NOT YET PROVED)

**Target: economically interpretable agreement-viability region and sign-correction rule, not another fitted witness.**

Let alpha=2−eta, beta=1−rho, delta=rho−eta; write the symmetric SW design root z in (0,1), satisfying
kappa(1−z) = 6 beta alpha / ((alpha−beta*z)*(alpha+2 beta*z)^3).

At the common fragmentation origin under SU g=0, the *direct, private-design-fixed* member welfare effect follows analytically from Cournot:
D(theta,z) = rho/(alpha+2 beta*z)^2
             −2 delta*(1+2 beta*z)/(alpha+2 beta*z)^3
             −6 delta*alpha / ((alpha−beta*z)*(alpha+2 beta*z)^3).
(This reproduces +0.0067679241288 at theta0.)

Firm-Nash comparative statics x'(0)=−F_x^(-1) F_g (SU's two-dimensional unilateral root) give
R(theta,z) = [sum_j CS_A,x_j*x'_j + sum_{j≠A} Pi_A,x_j*x'_j]_at_g=0.
The **local first-order warning condition** is
D(theta,z)>0 and R(theta,z)<−D(theta,z).
At theta0, D≈+0.0067679241 and R≈−0.0150697441, so the local member welfare direction reverses strictly. The same structure can be applied to the C admission margin at the origin (with a graph-symmetry factor: its derivative there equals twice the member derivative), but that identity itself is not new.

## 6. Proposed full-paper contribution language (EN, manuscript-facing, SOURCE-SCOPED)

> This paper studies whether endogenous product design changes the **feasibility of international standards agreements**, rather than merely the market outcomes following standardization. Technical compatibility is chosen by governments, while firms subsequently select costly characteristics outside the standard and compete in three integrated product markets. The resulting changes in domestic consumer surplus and globally earned producer rents enter national participation decisions. We establish a controlled contrast under a fixed bilateral-first accession procedure: an agreement that is attainable when firms' nonstandardized designs are restricted to their fragmented-market levels becomes unattainable at every positive compatibility depth when firms may reoptimize. Yet at a positive depth the fully optimized international allocation raises every country's welfare relative to fragmentation. The economic lesson is that **conditional gains from a common standard do not imply sequential implementability**. The underlying private-design response alters country-specific continuation values, and the outsider's intermediate option interacts with the bargaining agenda to prevent agreement. Our results are a certified, institution-specific existence demonstration, not a claim of universal standards failure or policymaker irrationality.
