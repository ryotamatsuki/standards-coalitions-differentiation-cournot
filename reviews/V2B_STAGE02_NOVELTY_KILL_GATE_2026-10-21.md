## 1. Question, contribution unit, and null hypothesis

Question: Can a government's promised international compatibility arrangement be made **less** attractive to governments solely because firms subsequently re-optimize their separately chosen product similarity, even when compatibility raises network utility?

Original five-stage game: A offers AB SU and continuous compatibility depth g to B; B approves or SW; C may apply; A and B approve unanimously with **unchanged g** for IS; each of 3 home firms subsequently chooses residual similarity x_i at convex design cost; firms sell Cournot quantities in each of 3 identical national markets. National welfare includes domestic CS, home-firm world operating profits and its one-time design investment.
Matrices: B=rho*H+(1-rho)*X; H is standard-compatibility matrix, X_ij=(x_i+x_j)/2; fulfilled expectation utility includes eta*H; Stage 5 q=[I+B-eta*H]^{-1}*(a-c)1 only where positive. This is a model-specific quadratic/Cournot demand architecture.

Headline witness (restricted): eta=379/500, rho=9/10, kappa=1701/2000, a-c=1 and nonempty unspecified open neighborhood. Rigorous interval program reports no positive-depth standards union or IS when firms reoptimize, but at least one positive-depth welfare-superior, mutually acceptable IS when firms' x are exogenously frozen at SW optimum. This is a **comparison of separate counterfactual games**, NOT a logical contradiction of a published theorem and NOT evidence of a generic derivative sign.

Null to try to prove: existing work already gives the same policy ranking or the restricted witness as a corollary under a relabeling, stage relocation, or a generic indirect-welfare effect. The burden is on V2b.

**Direct high-priority predecessor discoveries**:
- Economides & Flyer 1998 already makes technical-standard coalition competition vs external network effects the central phenomenon. It uses FIRM standards affiliation and Cournot, not ex-post design investment induced by state commitments.
- Gandal & Shy 2001 already studies GOVERNMENT standard recognition, SU excluding C, network effects and costs. **The full model/propositions are not accessible** in current search; absence of a described V2b claim from abstract is NO EVIDENCE of novelty.
- Baake & Boom 2001 already separates inherent product quality from standard compatibility and shows firms can achieve adapter compatibility through endogenous quality choices. Their game is quality -> adapter -> price, not government agreement -> quality -> Cournot. Reversed timing may be substantively essential, but exact theorem absorption pending.
- Takarada et al 2020 already establishes member–outsider welfare tension, a regional-versus-global standards result for 3 symmetric governments, and failed moderate compromises (Proposition 6, Corollary) with negative externalities / core.
- Takarada & Kawabata 2025 is the most pressing recent institutional comparison: continuous standards, three-country Cournot, regional/global MR, AND §5.4 differentiating goods to modify regional coalition preferences. The differentiation in that discussion is **exogenous**, unlike V2b post-policy Nash selection; their standard internalizes negative externality and is tied to compliance cost, unlike V2b network compatibility.
- Hashimzade & Sun 2026 introduces government policy + two R&D investment channels + network Cournot and welfare reversal. Two countries, subsidy/tax rather than harmonization coalition; no A/B commitment/C accession.
- Toshimitsu (2016, 2018) already relates the strength of network compatibility to product substitutability, preventing any broad novelty claim for eta-rho comparisons.
- Rodrigues 2026 describes broader linear-demand endogenous design based on product characteristics, creating a possible generic parent-class concern for V2b's additive cross-effects.
