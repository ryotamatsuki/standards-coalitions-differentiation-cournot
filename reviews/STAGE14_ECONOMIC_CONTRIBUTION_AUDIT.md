## What counts as a theory contribution here?

The standard applied in this audit is a new explanation or prediction about economically meaningful choices and outcomes, supported by a correct, interpretable argument and distinguished from the closest existing results. New algebra, an extra endogenous variable, a new timing convention, or an exact numerical threshold are insufficient on their own. A simple model can make a significant contribution; a complex proof can certify an unimportant claim.

This is an audit framework rather than a journal's formal eight-part scorecard. Its primary grounding is RIO's official [Aims and Scope](https://link.springer.com/journal/11151/aims-and-scope): substantive IO/policy theory and methods understandable to nonspecialists, alongside interest in observable economic conditions. Varian's author-hosted [*How to Build an Economic Model in Your Spare Time*](https://people.ischool.berkeley.edu/~hal/Papers/how.pdf), sections 1.2, 1.4–1.7, emphasizes insight, economic interest, stripping a model to its operative mechanism, recognizing general-theory predecessors, and independent explanation. This is methodological advice, not a necessary-and-sufficient acceptance test. It does not replace the model and proposition comparisons below.

## Three-sentence explanation test

1. Previous research established that standards alter network benefits, competition, product choices and governments' cooperation incentives, including noncoordination.
2. V2b shows that, under the **same bilateral-first accession game**, allowing firms to redesign residual attributes can remove every positive-depth agreement predicted by a frozen-design restriction, even though a direct grand continuation benefits all countries over fragmentation.
3. It changes how that class of agreements should be assessed: downstream private design and intermediate outside options can determine implementability, but cannot be inferred from final welfare gains alone; permitting a direct grand offer removes this obstruction in the audited example.

The last qualification is part of the knowledge gain, not a footnote to suppress. What an economist would fail to understand without this paper is at most this controlled, design-driven consent failure under a bounded class of procedures. The broad ideas of private reaction, incompatibility and coalition externalities do not depend on this paper.

## Generality threat ranking and two selected tests

1. **Agenda and consent restrictions — strongest threat.** The original equilibrium excludes direct grand offers, depth renegotiation and transfers. Selected test: derive direct-grand acceptance and show the existing no-union result is not stability against unrestricted coalition deviations. It fails broad institutional invariance, informatively.
2. **Additive technical/residual substitution mapping — second threat.** Selected test: PSD-preserving mixed kernel `B_e=(rho-e)H+(1-rho)X+e(H Hadamard X)`. Exact local signs survive e in [0,.001]. Global government nonformation in this kernel is NOT certified.
3. **Competition form and fulfilled expectations.** The amplification formula uses the stated Cournot demand system. No Bertrand portability is established. A full alternative competition model is not automatically necessary, but it is necessary before claiming competition-form independence.
4. **Three identical governments / once-paid quadratic design cost.** Symmetry drives edge-counting and the simple participation margins. No arbitrary asymmetric-country or cost-function theorem is available. Broader country/cost models were not added because they would not cure an unmotivated institution or source priority gap.

## Seven hostile-editor questions answered

1. **Correct but unimportant?** Not merely formal: an institutional forecast changes. Its wider importance is still insufficiently established.
2. **Just extra complexity?** The restricted/full comparison isolates a behavioral margin, but several ingredients are old; the compound implication must carry the contribution.
3. **New mechanism identified?** A candidate national-incidence-to-consent implication is identified. Generic strategic feedback is not new.
4. **Important institutional prediction changes?** Yes in the specified game: positive IS versus no positive union. General international fragmentation is not predicted.
5. **Artificial function-form outcome?** Not solely exact additivity locally, but not yet competition-form independent. The nonlinear fixed-depth failure is real.
6. **Would predecessor authors recognize new knowledge?** They might recognize the narrow same-agenda design restriction experiment as incremental. The source audit cannot guarantee their assessment or clear unavailable final propositions.
7. **Positive reason to send for review?** A concise causal theorem explaining when a conventional frozen-design policy assessment misclassifies consent, with honest institution boundaries. At present the positive case is a research prospect, not a closed Level3 gate.
