# Suggested manuscript form

Preferred, conditional: **regular theory article** (not automatically a 2,000-word letter), because the three layers—private response, national incidence, accession—need a defensible game sequence, tight predecessor comparisons, and a rigorous Appendix.

1. Introduction: Private adaptation flexibility can unexpectedly impair agreements.
2. Closest literature and contrast: Barrett–Yang's technical compliance redesign costs versus residual design response; Lambertini–Rossini and Cabral–Salant; Aghion et al. on protocols.
3. Baseline economy: inherited original V2b without alteration.
4. One extension: old-architecture adjustment cost lambda(x-z)^2/2 and once-paid welfare accounting.
5. Main theorem: local unique critical lambda with exact Nash M-matrix monotonic proof and rational witness.
6. Institution boundary: at lambda=0 direct grand offers remain acceptable; result is sequential-protocol-conditional.
7. Economic and observable predictions, limitations, conclusion.
Appendix: Nash regularity, exact endpoint verification, nonempty parameter neighborhood if independently proved, protocols and source matrix.

Suggested title: **When Product-Design Flexibility Undermines International Standards Agreements**.

Suggested abstract: In a three-country standards-accession game, we introduce a single cost of adjusting residual product attributes away from an existing design. Firms' post-commitment design choices can undermine governmental consent to technical compatibility even when a direct multilateral arrangement benefits all countries. We show at a certified primitive configuration that the marginal consent incentive increases strictly with design-adjustment curvature, crosses zero once, and thereby changes the feasibility of sequential standards accession. The result is generated by firm-induced consumer and rival-profit incidence, not by technical compliance costs. The critical value is local to low-depth agreements; broader priority and institutional claims require separate validation.
