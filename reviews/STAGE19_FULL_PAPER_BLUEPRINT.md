## Stage19 abstract candidate (NOT submission-ready)
We examine how countries choose the depth of international technical standards when firms subsequently select the similarity of product attributes that remain outside the standard. A three-country Cournot model separates technical compatibility from residual product positioning and embeds both private design choices and sovereign accession decisions in a sequential game. We derive an explicit Nash design continuation and provide primitive sufficient conditions under which private firms choose excessively similar residual characteristics. In the same parameter region, deeper technical standardization induces further private similarity and thereby lowers the national marginal welfare return to standardization relative to a frozen-design evaluation. We then characterize the government's constrained optimal depth and the role of the excluded country's accession payoff. In a rigorously certified parameter configuration, the unique government-optimal depth is the binding outsider-participation boundary, and the resulting three-country agreement strictly dominates every bilateral alternative for the proposer. The model illustrates an implementability gap between private product adjustment, national welfare and desirable compatibility. The novelty of the joint theoretical proposition relative to existing endogenous design, compatibility and international agreement literature remains under detailed review.
