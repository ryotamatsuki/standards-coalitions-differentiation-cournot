# Stage16 legacy design consistency and counterfactual

Proposed stage-0 interpretation: all countries' representative firms inherit a common residual product architecture z from previous investment, strictly BEFORE the international standards game. For the canonical comparison, set this predetermined inherited index equal to the symmetric unrestricted SW equilibrium design of the original lambda=0 model (approximately 0.718592707322), ensuring a controlled reference. The legacy z is not a second new design decision, not a government policy variable and not recalculated after firms observe the technical standard.

Then at any g=0 history, the penalty lambda(x_i-z)^2/2 is minimized at z. Because the incumbent original design Nash FOC is also zero at z, the SW design remains exactly z for all lambda>=0. Adding lambda strictly raises individual design curvature, preserving the inherited full-cube Rosen sufficient strict uniqueness criterion. Initial resource adjustment cost is zero. For policy-depth derivatives at g=0, derivative lambda(x_i-z)x_i'(0)=0; consequently the direct adjustment-cost effect vanishes first order and local government-incidence changes arise from suppressed Nash responses, NOT a newly added standard-compliance charge.

At positive g, firms' designs deviate from z and actual adjustment COSTS are included in profits/welfare. The shortcut 'only behavioural effects' is NOT licensed at g>0. The scale and empirical observability of x remain a reduced-form interpretation and the choice z=zSW(theta0) is intentionally calibrated to isolate the counterfactual: it is not an equilibrium prediction of an explicit historical investment game. No multi-period structure has been introduced.

Crucially rho (technical weight), eta (network utility intensity), kappa (original design curvature), lambda (adjustment curvature) must not be conflated. Lambda is not cost of complying with a new interface standard. This distinguishes the named cost from Barrett–Yang switching to an international standard and from Grossman–McCalman–Staiger's expense of maintaining multiple destination-market versions. These differences are economic interpretations, not proof of independent novelty.
