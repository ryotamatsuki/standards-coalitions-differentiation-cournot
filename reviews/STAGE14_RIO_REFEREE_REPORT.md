## Current decision

**Level 2 — NARROW CONTRIBUTION; REOPEN before a Level 3 claim.** The work has more economic content than a novel threshold: private design changes national participation payoffs, and a controlled government-game prediction reverses. Nevertheless, the present evidence does not yet establish enough independent economic scope to call the paper RIO-ready. The positive case for sending it to referees is conceivable and concrete; it is not yet secured by the proof certificates. This judgment does not reject linear Cournot, require general demand, or interpret every unavailable predecessor as an actual absorption.

The most defensible question is: **When does ignoring post-agreement private product redesign misclassify national consent under a bilateral-first accession procedure?** The paper does not currently answer why actual international standards generally remain regional, or establish a stable partial coalition. At the principal witness, the endogenous outcome is fragmentation; the frozen-design prediction is international union. The word “stability” must refer to the specified extensive-form participation constraints, not arbitrary coalition-proof or core stability.

## Seven mandatory editorial questions

1. **Mathematically correct but economically unimportant?** That remains a live risk. The meaningful result is an agreement-feasibility reversal, not a small change in a numerical cutoff. The current witness and locally certified region do not establish its prevalence or economic magnitude.
2. **Only complicated existing theory?** Much of the building material is established. Lambertini–Rossini already combine government standards and endogenous product/technology choices; Gandal–Shy already have three-country standards coalitions. The residual claim must be the exact controlled redesign-to-consent contrast, with the local national-incidence explanation. More moving parts do not prove independence.
3. **An identified new mechanism?** A candidate is identified: firms save differentiation expenditure and alter competition after technical compatibility; the induced consumer and rival-export-profit effects change national consent. The generic chain-rule identity and Nash feedback are standard tools. Their particular institutional consequence is the possible contribution.
4. **Important institutional/policy prediction changes?** Yes inside the specified experiment: frozen-design international union versus endogenous fragmentation despite a Pareto-improving conditional international allocation. No general prediction about ISO, regionalism, or coalition stability follows.
5. **Artifact of Cournot or the affine map?** Not established as an artifact, but not cleared generally. Two certified maps establish two cases. The nonlinear frozen-design comparison fails at the initially specified depth and passes at a smaller one, so quantitative robustness is limited. The agenda restriction is at least as threatening as the competition form.
6. **Would the nearest predecessors recognize a new insight?** Possibly, if the full published-version comparison confirms that the exact result is absent. It is not defensible to claim first integration of endogenous product choice and government standards noncoordination. Unread final propositions remain unresolved, not evidence of priority.
7. **A positive reason to send to referees?** A transparent demonstration that partial-equilibrium fixed-design standardization assessments can misclassify individually acceptable agreements, even with fully rational governments and a common bargaining agenda. To become persuasive, that insight needs a better specified class of economically relevant agendas and a complete nearest-literature comparison.

## English editorial report (under 400 words)

The manuscript asks whether post-standardization private product redesign changes which international standards agreements national governments can accept. Its strongest result is a controlled comparison: the government agenda, market primitives, quantity stage, and design-cost accounting remain unchanged, while the firms’ residual designs are either frozen at their fragmentation equilibrium or freely reoptimized. At the certified witness, the restricted-design game supports international agreement, whereas the full game implements no positive-depth agreement. A conditional international allocation nevertheless benefits every country relative to fragmentation.

This is an economic result rather than merely a novel numerical threshold. The local analytical propositions help explain it: member firms reduce residual differentiation more than the outsider, and induced consumer and exporter-profit effects can overturn a positive direct compatibility benefit. However, the chain-rule decomposition and strategic-design feedback are not independently novel methods. The contribution must reside in their implication for national consent under the stated accession procedure.

I do not regard linear Cournot as a reason for rejection. Recent RIO theory illustrates that disciplined simple models can yield useful insights. My concerns are instead substantive. Uniform certified center bounds now imply all-depth persistence on a nonexplicit open primitive neighborhood; the explicit parameter certificate concerns local signs. This remains a model-specific existence result. More importantly, the bilateral-first, inherited-depth agenda excludes direct multilateral agreement and renegotiation. The paper therefore establishes an agenda-dependent implementability problem, not a general theory of stable regional standardization. Its nonlinear comparison also fails at the original fixed depth, which must remain visible.

Priority is not fully settled. Older research already integrates government standards with endogenous product or technology choices and obtains noncoordination. A first-integration claim would be incorrect. The exact redesign-to-national-consent contrast may survive, but unresolved published-version comparisons prevent unconditional novelty clearance.

My current classification is Level 2, a narrow substantive contribution. A Level 3 case would require a sharper class of economically relevant commitment institutions, the direct-grand-offer boundary made central to the claim, and a completed nearest-literature comparison. I recommend reopening those focused gates rather than adding arbitrary parameters or treating proof complexity as evidence of importance.

## Theory-researcher contribution statement (under 150 words)

In a three-country standards game, governments commit to compatibility before firms choose residual product similarity. Under a fixed bilateral-first accession procedure, freezing designs at their fragmentation equilibrium can predict an international agreement that disappears when designs are endogenous, although a conditional international allocation remains Pareto superior to fragmentation. Local sufficient conditions identify the incidence mechanism: members reduce residual differentiation more strongly than the outsider, and induced consumer and exporter-profit losses outweigh a positive direct compatibility benefit. Uniform center certificates imply all-depth persistence on a nonexplicit open primitive neighborhood; a separate explicit region supports the local mechanism. The candidate contribution is an agenda-dependent failure of fixed-design agreement assessment, not the first theory of endogenous standards, generic strategic incompatibility, or universal fragmentation.

## 学部3年生・懐疑的研究者への回答

「似ている程度を変えたら条件が変わる」だけなら、確かに論文の中心としては弱いです。今回確かめたいのは、それによって政府が成立させられる協定の予測まで逆になるか、という点です。

このモデルでは、企業の製品設計を固定すると、二国で始めた協定を三国に広げられます。ところが同じ政策と交渉手順でも、協定後に企業が製品を設計し直せるようにすると、正の深度の協定が成立しなくなる例が得られています。政府が判断を間違えるのではありません。企業の反応を省いた分析者の予測が変わる、という比較です。

なぜか。互換性は消費者に利益を与える一方、企業には製品を差別化するための費用を節約する誘因も生みます。証明した範囲では、協定参加国の企業は域外国の企業より強く、残った製品属性を似せる方向へ動きます。この変化が消費者の利益と自国企業の輸出利益に影響し、互換性だけを見た参加メリットを打ち消すことがあります。さらに域外国には「加入せず、二国間協定の外にいる」という選択肢があるので、全員がバラバラでいるより三国協定のほうが望ましくても、途中の加入判断では断られます。

役に立つ可能性があるのは、技術をつなげた効果だけで協定成立を評価しても、企業の製品変更と途中の選択肢を考慮すると結論が違い得る、と示す点です。ただし、今の段階では「国際標準化が進まない一般的な理由を解明した」とまでは言えません。三国が最初から直接交渉できれば、この例の望ましい協定は成立できます。特定の交渉手順への依存がどれほど現実的で、既存研究からどれほど独立しているかを詰めて初めて、掲載に値する重要性を強く主張できます。

## Required versus optional editorial work

**Required before Level 3:** (i) close or explicitly decide material published-version priority risks; (ii) identify a meaningful class of commitment/admission rules and state the direct-international-offer boundary; (iii) distinguish the explicit local region from the nonexplicit full-depth neighborhood; (iv) explain the national consumer/export-profit incidence in plain English with one controlled-outcome table; (v) call the current canonical response reduced differentiation, not re-differentiation; (vi) map observable technical and residual design choices without claiming an institutional anecdote validates the theorem.

**Optional:** a second competition form, arbitrary country count, general differentiable substitution maps, new independent design dimensions, or a larger parameter search. None is automatically needed. They should be undertaken only if a specified unresolved inference depends on them.
