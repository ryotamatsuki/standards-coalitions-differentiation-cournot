## C. What is actually learned

Technical standardization can leave firms free to alter product features outside the standard. In the audited sufficient region, the two firms connected by a new technical link respond more strongly than the outsider, and all reduce residual differentiation. Their private optimality does not neutralize national consequences: the own firm's first-order profit effect vanishes, while domestic consumer-surplus effects and rivals' effects on home exporters remain. Those induced losses can outweigh a direct compatibility gain.

The institutional implication is sharper than “outsiders have vetoes.” Holding designs at their fragmented-market levels supports a positive-depth international agreement under the specified governmental game. Allowing firms to optimize their residual attributes can instead eliminate every positive-depth path through the same bilateral-first inherited-depth procedure at the certified witness. Yet a direct international allocation benefits every country relative to fragmentation. The relevant obstacle is the change in **continuation outside options**, not the absence of a global benefit or irrational governments.

The independent new local propositions explain the sign reversal; the old all-depth theorem establishes a completed game outcome. Neither substitutes for the other. This is one connected contribution, not three independent discoveries.

## D. Contribution statement for an Introduction

The following proposed text is conditional on the source limits documented in the matrix and the explicit erratum. It makes no first/unique/universal priority claim.

> This paper studies how firms’ adjustment of product characteristics affects the formation of international standards agreements. We distinguish technical compatibility, chosen by governments, from residual horizontal differentiation, chosen subsequently by firms. Three nationally owned firms compete in three segmented Cournot markets, while governments value domestic consumer surplus and their home firms’ worldwide profits. A bilateral agreement can expand through accession at its inherited compatibility depth. This setting connects product-design incentives to the continuation payoffs that determine governmental consent.
>
> We first characterize the private-design equilibrium and its response to compatibility. On a regular interior symmetric branch, the direction of the design response depends on the balance between technical substitution and network benefits. At the fragmented benchmark, explicit sufficient conditions imply that a bilateral compatibility increase reduces differentiation by both members and the outsider, with a stronger response by member firms. These conditions describe a genuine equilibrium response on a nonempty parameter region; they are not assertions about every admissible parameter or every compatibility depth.
>
> We then derive an incidence condition connecting these private responses to national participation incentives. A firm’s own design first-order condition removes its own marginal profit effect, but it does not remove domestic consumer-surplus effects or the consequences of rival firms’ redesign for the home exporter. We identify sufficient conditions under which these induced losses exceed a positive direct compatibility gain, reversing the local participation assessment obtained when designs are held fixed. This result explains how optimizing firms can change the feasibility of a governmental agreement without a private optimization failure.
>
> Finally, we connect this mechanism to a completed institutional outcome. At an explicitly specified parameter vector, a computer-assisted proof covers every positive compatibility depth: an international agreement is attainable in the restricted fixed-design game, whereas no positive-depth union forms when firms reoptimize. Nevertheless, a hypothetical international agreement at a stated depth raises every country’s welfare relative to fragmentation. The result concerns the specified bilateral-first accession procedure and its continuation outside options, rather than a general impossibility of beneficial standardization. An analytically solved benchmark in which technical substitution and network effects balance yields maximum-depth international agreement, isolating the role of policy-induced redesign in the comparison.

## F. Strongest recommendation and rejection, compared

**Recommendation:** Readers of standards policy learn that treating product features outside a formal interface as fixed can misclassify the feasible agreement set. The paper gives a transparent private-design mechanism, sufficient local conditions on a nonempty primitive region, and a completed all-depth institutional counterexample with reproducible proof. The exact same governmental rule produces different outcomes depending on firms' post-policy design freedom, while a direct grand allocation remains attractive relative to fragmentation. This is coherent economic content, not a list of familiar components.

**Rejection:** Comparable private-investment feedback and standards/coalition inefficiencies are already known. The strongest global outcome remains parameter-specific and depends on a restrictive inherited-depth agenda without renegotiation or transfers; the exact certified local region is small. It has not been demonstrated that this precise protocol governs the real institutions suggested as motivation. Unavailable published versions could reveal closer overlap. A numerical certificate cannot by itself make these implications economically important.

**Judgment:** The new B/C results materially answer PR #6's objection that the paper offered only an unexplained single-witness computation. They do not eliminate the scope and priority objections. Hence CONDITIONAL GO is warranted; neither unconditional FULL-PAPER GO nor a blanket NO-GO based on simple Cournot primitives follows. A research note remains a sensible fallback if the remaining full-text comparisons absorb the residual contribution or if the author chooses a narrow diagnostic treatment. It is not compelled merely by the functional form.

Two further open published RIO checks were retrieved by the lead reviewer in this audit:

* Wang and Zhao, *On Horizontal Mergers in the Presence of Economies of Scale*, published 17 October 2026, DOI [10.1007/s11151-026-10069-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11151-026-10069-x): linear homogeneous Cournot demand, concave quadratic costs, closed-form equilibrium, merger-profitability and welfare parameter thresholds (Props1–3 and appendix). Its merger question does not provide a direct absorption theorem for V2b.
* Sorek, *Quality Innovation with Variable Demand Elasticity*, RIO68,311–325 (2026), DOI [10.1007/s11151-025-10046-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11151-025-10046-w): model, free-entry and small-number Cournot sections distinguish assumptions behind conditional comparative statics. This is a more general framework, but publication of both papers gives no basis for making generality a universal gate.

These comparisons support a manuscript organized around economic learning and explicit sign conditions. They do not imply that an arbitrary number of propositions, an elementary design root, or a larger state space is needed. No journal-wide probability or author-intent inference is drawn.
