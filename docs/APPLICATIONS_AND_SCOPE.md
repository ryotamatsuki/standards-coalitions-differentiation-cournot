# Empirical interpretation, examples, and limits

## Precise scope of current demand primitives
Study **network-dependent goods subject to design-constraining compatibility standards**: harmonization raises usefulness of having a large interoperable installed base AND narrows the permissible range of product features, raising substitutability among competing products.

This is **not** a model of all technical standards. It tightly links network complementarity and product homogeneity through the same `z_i z_j` terms. The government policy `g` is a **minimum homogeneity floor**, not independent plug-and-play compatibility.

## Illustrative sectors (hypotheses, not calibrations)
1. **Standard exchange pallets (e.g., EPAL / EUR pallet):** standard size and construction enable interchange and pooling; constrained design can increase basic functional substitutability. **Limit:** standards are heavily administered by private industry bodies, not the game's bilateral government accession procedure. Value derives from installed pallet stock / logistics acceptance rather than just current-period output.
2. **ISO intermodal freight containers:** standardized dimensions/corner fittings expand transport-handling network usefulness and standardize important core physical attributes. **Limit:** global ISO frameworks are not clean A–B then C bargaining; container types and value-added features vary.
3. **European rail signalling / train-control interoperability (ERTMS/ETCS):** national interoperability policy with public governance and cross-border network benefits. **Limit:** procurement and certification dominate, and final equipment may remain differentiated; pure Cournot quantity competition is a stylization.
4. **Poor fit: USB-C and many telecommunications interface standards:** compatible interfaces need not homogenize the entire downstream product; the model's unitary linkage of substitution and interoperability would overstate product convergence.

## Why reality may show SU while the zero-cost symmetric model does not
- Government bargaining is rarely ex ante symmetric; countries differ in market size, installed bases, technology, national champions, existing standards and switching cost.
- Standards can restrict market access or require multi-homing; baseline permits sales of every good in all three national markets regardless of standard.
- Real compatibility benefits often depend on installed base, equipment complements, accumulated adapters and service networks, not contemporaneous quantities.
- Regional standardization can create geographic and regulatory clusters or coordination rents not captured by this payoff system.
- The baseline has no compliance investment or standard transition cost and no separate product-characteristic differentiation dimension.

## Interpretation discipline
Do not state as empirically established that these industries satisfy `B_ij=H_ij=z_i z_j` or that harmonization directly causes lower prices in every equilibrium. Distinguish (i) engineering interoperability; (ii) economic substitutability; (iii) actual network utility. The current model **imposes a link**, and robustness to separating the variables is an open research question.
