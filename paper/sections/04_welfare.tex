\section{Welfare and the Participation Wedge}\label{sec:welfare}

Proposition~\ref{prop:rejection} implies that the actual
government game implements no positive-depth technical union.
This political outcome need not coincide with the welfare
ranking of the \emph{conditional} market allocations that
would follow an international agreement.

\begin{proposition}[A Pareto-improving international standard is blocked]
\label{prop:pareto}
At the same exact witness $\theta_0$, impose the
hypothetical international compatibility depth
$g=1/20$ and allow firms to choose their true
post-policy design Nash equilibrium. Then every
country's welfare is strictly greater than at the
actual separate-standards continuation:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pareto}
 i(1/20)-s >0.
\end{equation}
This allocation is unavailable as a positive-depth
subgame-perfect outcome of the specified government game.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At this inherited depth, a separate rational-interval
calculation isolates the unique international design
root, obtains the corresponding fulfilled-expectations
Cournot quantities from \eqref{eq:Mq}, and evaluates
national welfare by \eqref{eq:welfare}. The intervals
prove a strictly positive gap:
\[
 i(1/20)-s \in
 [0.0169346278736,\;0.0169346279395]\quad (>0).
\]
Symmetry makes the comparison valid for all three countries.
Proposition~\ref{prop:rejection} independently excludes
the requisite positive-depth government consent for
\emph{every} $g>0$, not just the tested depth.
\end{proof}

At $g=.05$, the three-country $\IS$ raises total
welfare relative to $\SW$ by approximately $0.050804$
in the model's normalized units, while a bilateral
$\SU$ raises total welfare by approximately $0.017234$.
Yet each $\SU$ member loses approximately $0.000473$

It is precisely this intermediate coalition payoff
pattern that makes the sequential agreement impossible.

\subsection{The role of endogenous nonstandardized attributes}

The government does not maximize a private firm's
design profit; it additionally counts national
consumer surplus and the consequences of \emph{rivals'}
design changes. At the fragmented benchmark $g=0$,
the member/outsider designs in the $\SU$ continuation
satisfy
\[
 u'(0)\simeq0.060810232,\qquad
 v'(0)\simeq0.032304817 .
\]
Increasing compatibility therefore induces the firms
to choose somewhat greater \emph{residual homogeneity}
and incur less differentiation expenditure, rather than
automatically forcing whole-product standardization.

The local member-welfare derivative
\eqref{eq:member-path} separates an immediate
compatibility change at fixed designs from the
result of the firms' strategic response:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:welfare-decomp}
 \left.m'(g)\right|_{g=0}
 =
 \underbrace{0.0067679241}_{\text{direct policy effect}}
 -
 \underbrace{0.0150697441}_{\text{design-induced effect}}
 \simeq -0.0083018200.
\end{equation}
The direct term itself reflects a positive change in
domestic consumer surplus (about $0.346626$)
almost cancelled by a negative effect on the
home exporting firm's net income (about $-0.339858$).
The further design response is negative in both
these national welfare components at the witness.
Crucially, a firm does not choose a first-order
loss from its \emph{own} optimally selected design:
its Nash first-order condition is
$\Pi_{A,x_A}=0$. Along the symmetric $\SU$
design path $(x_A,x_B,x_C)=(u,u,v)$,
write $W_A=\CS+\Pi_A$ and use the envelope
condition to separate the induced effects:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:member-envelope}
\begin{aligned}
\left.\frac{dW_A}{dg}\right|_{0}
={}&W_{A,g}
+\underbrace{(\CS_{x_A}+\CS_{x_B})u'
              +\CS_{x_C}v'}_{\text{domestic consumer response}}\\
&+\underbrace{\Pi_{A,x_B}u'
              +\Pi_{A,x_C}v'}_{\text{rivals' effect on exporter profit}},
\qquad \Pi_{A,x_A}=0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
At the exact witness these two design-induced
components are approximately $-0.005216220$
and $-0.009853524$, totaling $-0.015069744$
in \eqref{eq:welfare-decomp}. The negative
national response therefore arises from the
change in consumer surplus and the impact of
\emph{competitors'} designs on home-firm
worldwide profits, not a loss a firm chooses
from its own marginal redesign. The general incidence
formula is given in \eqref{eq:analytic-loss}, where both
induced-loss components are added; the local sign condition
\eqref{eq:analytic-C-conditions} holds on a certified
nonempty parameter region. This is logically distinct from
the all-depth result in Proposition~\ref{prop:rejection}.

At the symmetric origin, the marginal $C$ admission
effect is twice the member policy effect by
edge-counting symmetry. That identity is \emph{not}
a second independent novel mechanism. The economic
content lies in how downstream private design changes
the sign of the government's reduced welfare margin,
while the particular agenda creates a separate outsider
opportunity. This is a diagnosis of a potential
participation failure, not a claim that compulsory
standards, transfers, or mandates have been optimally
designed or implemented.
