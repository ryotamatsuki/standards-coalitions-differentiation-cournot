\section{Sequential Compatibility and Coalition Formation}
\label{sec:results}

The central result concerns a \emph{difference in feasible
governmental agreements}, not a difference between two
fixed-design demand schedules. Every comparison below uses the
unique downstream design and Cournot equilibria derived in
Section~\ref{sec:equilibrium}, unless explicitly labelled
as a frozen-design counterfactual.

\begin{proposition}[No positive-depth agreement under endogenous design]
\label{prop:rejection}
Under the five-stage bilateral-first consent game, canonical
similarity mapping \eqref{eq:matrices}, and the exact witness
\eqref{eq:witness}, for every real $g\in(0,1]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rejection}
 m(g)<s,\qquad i(g)<o(g).
\end{equation}
Thus no substantive positive-depth $\SU$ or $\IS$ is reached
in a subgame-perfect outcome of the government game.
Formal zero-depth membership labels need not be unique.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write the symmetric bilateral-union design continuation as
$(u(g),u(g),v(g))$, with $u$ chosen by the members and $v$
by the outsider. Equation~\eqref{eq:design-bridge} specifies
the \emph{unilateral} firm Nash conditions
$F_M(u,v,g)=F_O(u,v,g)=0$.
As long as this Jacobian is nonsingular, implicit
differentiation gives the total member-welfare derivative
\begin{equation}\label{eq:member-path}
 m'(g)=W_{A,g}^{\SU}
       +W_{A,u}^{\SU}u'(g)
       +W_{A,v}^{\SU}v'(g),\qquad
 \begin{pmatrix}u'\\v'\end{pmatrix}
 =-D_{(u,v)}F^{-1}D_gF.
\end{equation}
Similarly, writing the unique $\IS$ design equilibrium
as $(y(g),y(g),y(g))$, the total accession-margin derivative is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:outsider-path}
 \bigl(i(g)-o(g)\bigr)'
 =i_g+i_y y'
 -\bigl(W_{C,g}^{\SU}
       +W_{C,u}^{\SU}u'+W_{C,v}^{\SU}v'\bigr),
 \qquad y'=-F_{I,g}/F_{I,y}.
\end{equation}
The derivatives $W_{A,g}$ and $i_g$ in these expressions hold
private design fixed, while the terms multiplied by $u',v',y'$
incorporate the firms' \emph{actual} post-policy responses.

The exact rational-interval certificate associated with
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} isolates the unique design Nash
uniformly and checks nonsingularity on \emph{all} of
$g\in[0,1]$. It then encloses the full right-hand sides
of \eqref{eq:member-path} and
\eqref{eq:outsider-path} over 1,000 adjacent closed
rational policy intervals, proving the strict uniform bounds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:signed-bounds}
  m'(g)<-0.0079701929973,\qquad
  \bigl(i(g)-o(g)\bigr)'<-0.0150388833377.
\end{equation}
These are conservative upper bounds returned by the interval
certificate, \emph{not} approximate maxima of a numerical grid.
At $g=0$, all three institutional labels deliver the same
market and firm-design continuation, so
$m(0)=o(0)=i(0)=s$. Integrating the strictly negative
derivatives yields \eqref{eq:rejection} for every $g>0$.

Backward induction is immediate but economically consequential:
$C$ strictly prefers the bilateral outsider continuation to
accession at any positive inherited $g$, hence does not apply;
anticipating a terminal $\SU$, $B$ strictly prefers rejecting
$A$'s positive-depth proposal and obtaining $\SW$.
Government $A$ cannot bypass these choices by selecting a
different $g$. A zero-depth formal union remains
payoff-equivalent to $\SW$ and may be supported by indifferent
votes.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The frozen-design forecast}

Define $\widehat{m}(g)$, $\widehat{o}(g)$ and
$\widehat{i}(g)$ by \eqref{eq:welfare} in a \emph{different}
restricted-strategy government game. In this benchmark,
all firms' designs are held at their common actual $\SW$
Nash level $\bar x=x^{\SW}$ after every history, even when
governments choose a positive compatibility depth. The cost
of that design remains the same once-paid real resource cost.
No firm can change design in the benchmark; the policy rules
and Cournot stage remain unchanged.

\begin{proposition}[A frozen-design policy forecast reverses the outcome]
\label{prop:frozen}
At $\theta_0$ under the canonical residual-similarity mapping,
the frozen-design game admits a positive-depth $\IS$
with the inherited compatibility $g=1/20$. At this offer,
$C$ strictly prefers applying, both $A$ and $B$ strictly
prefer approving accession, and the proposed $\IS$
beats $\SW$ and \emph{every possible terminal $\SU$
policy} for the proposer. Hence at least one optimal
government outcome in the restricted-design game is a
positive-depth $\IS$, unlike the original game in
Proposition~\ref{prop:rejection}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Opposite-sign rational interval bounds for the $\SW$
design FOC isolate
$\bar x\in[0.71859270731,0.71859270734]$.
Substituting the \emph{same} $\bar x$ into each
conditional government regime and taking $g=1/20$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frozen-votes}
 \widehat{i}(1/20)-\widehat{o}(1/20)
    >0.00023461662306,\quad
 \widehat{i}(1/20)>\widehat{m}(1/20),\quad
 \widehat{i}(1/20)>s.
\end{equation}
The strict comparison with all alternative bilateral outcomes
uses a separate entire-policy-domain interval envelope:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frozen-envelope}
 \sup_{g\in[0,1]}\widehat{m}(g)
       <2.423573565
       <2.4413970373
       <\widehat{i}(1/20).
\end{equation}
The full set $[0,1]$ in the supremum is certified using
500 adjacent rational intervals, not guessed by maximizing
over a grid. By \eqref{eq:frozen-votes}, $C$ applies and the
two existing members approve the stated feasible $\IS$;
$B$ also prefers it to declining the entire proposal.
Equation~\eqref{eq:frozen-envelope} excludes all terminal
$\SU$ offers and $\SW$ as optimal proposals for $A$.
With affirmative votes at indifference, the positive-depth
feasible-acceptance set is closed within the compact policy
domain; a proposer optimum exists. No particular
globally maximizing $g$ or uniquely labelled government
strategy profile is asserted.
\end{proof}

The reversal in Propositions~\ref{prop:rejection}
and \ref{prop:frozen} is a controlled contrast between
\emph{one original private-design game} and a
\emph{strategy-restricted forecast} evaluated under the
same government decision sequence and design-cost accounting.
It is not an experiment in which standards accidentally alter
the entirety of product quality, and it is not an
endogenous full-equilibrium result of the frozen design
restrictions themselves.
