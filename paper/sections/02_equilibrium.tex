\section{Market and Design Equilibria}\label{sec:equilibrium}

We solve the game backwards, first taking the realized institutional
regime $S\in\{\SW,\SU,\IS\}$, its chosen $g$, and the firms' design
vector $x$ as given. A Cournot firm takes expected network use
$n^e$ as fixed during a unilateral output deviation. The first-order
condition in a national market is
$p_i-c-q_i=0$. Substituting \eqref{eq:demand} and subsequently imposing
rationally fulfilled expectations $n^e=q$ yields the system
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Mq}
 M(S,g,x)q=d\mathbf{1},\qquad
 M=I+B-\eta H
 =I+(\rho-\eta)H+(1-\rho)X.
\end{equation}
This distinguishes the \emph{consumer} substitution matrix $B$
from the \emph{fulfilled-expectations Cournot} matrix $M$.
Write $\alpha=2-\eta$ and $\beta=1-\rho$.
At \eqref{eq:witness}, $\alpha=1.242$, $\beta=0.1$, the diagonal
of $M$ equals $\alpha$, and each off-diagonal element lies in
$[0,0.242]$.

\begin{lemma}[Unique interior quantity continuation]
\label{lem:quantities}
At $\theta_0$, for every $S$, $g\in[0,1]$, and
$x\in[0,1]^3$, each national Cournot game with fulfilled
network expectations has a unique equilibrium with $q_i>0$.
It is given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qinv}
 q(x;S,g)=dM(S,g,x)^{-1}\mathbf{1}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
With $d=1$, the (fulfilled-expectations) best-response fixed-point
map has components
\[
 T_i(q)=\max\left\{0,\,
 \frac{1-\sum_{j\ne i}M_{ij}q_j}{\alpha}\right\}.
\]
It maps $[0,1/\alpha]^3$ into itself, with sup-norm Lipschitz
constant at most $2(0.242)/1.242<1$. Moreover, every
untruncated numerator on that cube is at least
$1-2(0.242)/1.242>0$. Banach's fixed-point theorem
therefore gives a unique strictly interior nonnegative
continuation. The interior conditions coincide with
\eqref{eq:Mq}, proving \eqref{eq:qinv}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Private residual design}

Each firm anticipates \eqref{eq:qinv}, but chooses its own
$x_i$ while keeping rival firms' design choices fixed. Matrix
differentiation gives the indispensable unilateral-deviation
bridge
\begin{equation}\label{eq:design-bridge}
 \frac{\partial q}{\partial x_i}
 =-M^{-1}\frac{\partial M}{\partial x_i}q,\qquad
 F_i(x;S,g):=\frac{\partial\Pi_i}{\partial x_i}
 =6q_i\frac{\partial q_i}{\partial x_i}
       +\kappa(1-x_i).
\end{equation}
In particular, the symmetric equilibrium path cannot be
found by replacing a unilateral firm's derivative with a
derivative that moves all symmetric firms' designs jointly.
For a design in the interior, $F_i=0$; endpoint designs must
satisfy the appropriate one-sided inequalities.

\begin{lemma}[Unique global design continuation]
\label{lem:design}
For the canonical similarity mapping \eqref{eq:matrices}
at $\theta_0$, after every regime and every $g\in[0,1]$,
the Stage-4 design game has exactly one pure Nash equilibrium
$x^*(S,g)$ in $[0,1]^3$. The equilibrium is interior. Under
$\SW$ or $\IS$ all firms choose the same $x$; under $\SU$ the
two members choose a common $u$ and the outsider chooses $v$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each firm's design payoff is continuous on the compact product
strategy space. Differentiating \eqref{eq:design-bridge}, the
standard full-strategy Jacobian bound at this witness is
\[
  \left|\frac{\partial^2(3q_i^2)}
                    {\partial x_i\partial x_j}\right|
       \leq27(1-\rho)^2d^2=0.27 .
\]
The design-cost term subtracts $\kappa=0.8505$ from the
own-strategy diagonal. Therefore the symmetric part of
the Nash pseudo-gradient Jacobian is strictly
negative diagonally dominant, since
\[
 \kappa>3\cdot 27(1-\rho)^2d^2=0.81.
\]
Own-payoff strict concavity and Rosen's diagonal-strict-concavity
condition yield existence and uniqueness of a pure Nash
on the \emph{entire asymmetric cube} \citep{Rosen1965}.
Uniform interior root-inclusion checks for the member/outside
and international design equations give an interior Nash at
each $g$, including $g=0$ and $g=1$; the exact continuous
root inclusion and endpoint details are documented in
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} and the accompanying
rational-interval certificate. Symmetry and global uniqueness
then imply the stated member and international identities.
\end{proof}

The common $\SW$ solution at $g=0$ is
$x_A^{\SW}=x_B^{\SW}=x_C^{\SW}\simeq0.718592707322$.
Its welfare is $W^{\SW}\simeq2.422078421718$ per country.

\subsection{Reduced-form government continuation payoffs}

Denote, with firms truly reoptimizing in each regime,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gov-payoffs}
 s=W_i(\SW;x^*(\SW)),\quad
 m(g)=W_A(\SU,g;x^*(\SU,g))
     =W_B(\SU,g;x^*(\SU,g)),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gov-payoffs2}
 o(g)=W_C(\SU,g;x^*(\SU,g)),\qquad
 i(g)=W_j(\IS,g;x^*(\IS,g))\quad (j=A,B,C).
\end{equation}
These are the \emph{country} payoffs, not profits of a standard-setting
consortium. They encode the exact off-path alternatives:
$C$ compares $i(g)$ with $o(g)$ at inherited $g$,
and $B$ can reject the entire proposal to receive $s$.
The government equilibrium is obtained by backward induction
over these well-defined continuations, not by comparing
fixed-design firm profits at a single $g$.
