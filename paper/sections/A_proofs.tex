\section{Reduced Formulas and Computer-Assisted Proofs}\label{app:proofs}


\subsection{Positive semidefiniteness of demand}\label{app:utility-psd}
At the exact parameter witness, $B$ is affine in
$(x_A,x_B,x_C,g)$ for each regime, on $[0,1]^4$.
Every matrix in this cube is a convex combination
of the 16 vertex matrices. Direct exact-rational
principal-minor checks find all principal minors
nonnegative at every vertex (the minimum determinant
is $243/250$ for $\SW$ and zero for $\SU$ and $\IS$).
Hence all vertex matrices are positive semidefinite;
convexity of the positive-semidefinite cone proves
$B\succeq 0$ on the full cube. For the alternative
$\phi(x)=x^2$, the variables $z_i=x_i^2\in[0,1]$
cover the same cube and $B$ is affine in $(z,g)$,
so the same argument applies. This does not
establish strict concavity of demand at every endpoint.
\subsection{A full-strategy curvature bound}\label{app:rosen-bound}

The canonical linear similarity mapping permits an analytic
global firm-Nash uniqueness certificate.
Set $\alpha=621/500$, $\beta=1/10$,
$\overline m=121/500$, and
$D_0=\alpha-2\overline m=379/500>0$.
For every policy regime, depth $g\in[0,1]$ and
asymmetric design vector $x\in[0,1]^3$,
the Cournot matrix $M$ has diagonal $\alpha$
and nonnegative off-diagonal entries no greater
than $\overline m$. Strict row diagonal dominance
implies $\|M^{-1}\|_\infty\leq D_0^{-1}$,
while its positive fixed-point quantities satisfy
$\|q\|_\infty\leq\alpha^{-1}$.

Write $M_i=\partial M/\partial x_i$ and
$q^{[i]}=\partial q/\partial x_i$.
For the linear mapping,
$\|M_i\|_\infty\leq\beta$ and
$\partial M_i/\partial x_j=0$.
Thus matrix differentiation, applied to the
\emph{full} asymmetric design space, yields
\[
 q^{[i]}=-M^{-1}M_iq,\qquad
 \frac{\partial q^{[i]}}{\partial x_j}
 =M^{-1}M_jM^{-1}M_iq+
  M^{-1}M_iM^{-1}M_jq.
\]
In particular,
\[
 \|q^{[i]}\|_\infty
 \leq\frac{\beta}{D_0\alpha}<0.106221,
 \quad
 \left\|\frac{\partial q^{[i]}}{\partial x_j}
 \right\|_\infty
 \leq\frac{2\beta^2}{D_0^2\alpha}<0.028027.
\]
By the product rule, every second derivative of
each firm's operating profit $3q_k^2$ obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:global-design-curvature}
 \left|
 \frac{\partial^2(3q_k^2)}
      {\partial x_i\partial x_j}
 \right|
 \leq 6\left[
 \left(\frac{\beta}{D_0\alpha}\right)^2
 +\frac{2\beta^2}{D_0^2\alpha^2}
 \right]<0.203092=:h.
\end{equation}
The once-paid cost adds $-\kappa$ to the
pseudo-gradient's own Jacobian diagonal but
nothing to its off-diagonals. Therefore the
symmetric part of the three-player pseudo-gradient
Jacobian is strictly negative diagonally dominant:
its largest row upper bound is at most
$-\kappa+3h<-0.2412$.
This verifies Rosen strict diagonal concavity,
as well as each firm's own-payoff strict concavity,
\emph{globally}, without using symmetry to
remove unilateral deviations.
Existence of the required \emph{interior} Nash
for each policy depth is separately certified
by the uniform root-inclusion program below.

\subsection{SU market reduction and unilateral design equations}

After establishing global firm-Nash uniqueness, the SU continuation
is symmetric between A and B: $x_A=x_B=u$, $x_C=v$. Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app_mn}
 \alpha=2-\eta,\quad \beta=1-\rho,\quad
 h=(\rho-\eta)g+\beta u,\quad
 n=\beta(u+v)/2,\quad D=\alpha(\alpha+h)-2n^2.
\end{equation}
Then the matrix in \eqref{eq:Mq} and its reduced solution are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app_market}
 M_{\SU}=\begin{pmatrix}
 \alpha & h & n\\ h & \alpha & n\\ n & n & \alpha
 \end{pmatrix},\quad
 (\alpha+h)Q_M+nQ_O=1,\quad 2nQ_M+\alpha Q_O=1,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app_quantities}
 Q_M=\frac{\alpha-n}{D},\qquad
 Q_O=\frac{\alpha+h-2n}{D}.
\end{equation}
These are market quantities only; the design Nash conditions require
\emph{unilateral}, not joint symmetric, derivatives:
\begin{align}
 F_M(u,v,g)
 &=\left[6q_A(-M^{-1}M_{x_A}q)_A+
                  \kappa(1-x_A)\right]_{x=(u,u,v)}=0,
 \label{eq:app_Fm}\\
 F_O(u,v,g)
 &=\left[6q_C(-M^{-1}M_{x_C}q)_C+
                  \kappa(1-x_C)\right]_{x=(u,u,v)}=0.
 \label{eq:app_Fo}
\end{align}
The correct domestic SU surplus and country welfare terms are
\begin{align}
 \CS_{\SU}&=(1+\rho g+\beta u)Q_M^2+
                    Q_O^2/2+2nQ_MQ_O,\label{eq:app_cs}\\
 W_M&=\CS_{\SU}+3Q_M^2-\kappa(1-u)^2/2,\label{eq:app_wm}\\
 W_O&=\CS_{\SU}+3Q_O^2-\kappa(1-v)^2/2.\label{eq:app_wo}
\end{align}
The substitution term in consumer surplus uses $\rho g$,
whereas the Cournot off-diagonal matrix uses $(\rho-\eta)g$.
Mixing them would change the national welfare object and
invalidate the main result.

\subsection{IS scalar design equation}

For the unique symmetric international continuation put
$t=(\rho-\eta)g+\beta y$ and $Q_I=1/(\alpha+2t)$.
A single firm's deviation from a symmetric design profile yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app_isf}
 F_I(y,g)=\kappa(1-y)-
  \frac{6\beta\alpha}{(\alpha-t)(\alpha+2t)^3}=0.
\end{equation}
International per-country welfare then reduces to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app_iw}
 W_I(y,g)=
 \frac{\tfrac92+3(\rho g+\beta y)}{(\alpha+2t)^2}
 -\frac{\kappa}{2}(1-y)^2 .
\end{equation}
Equations \eqref{eq:app_Fm}--\eqref{eq:app_iw} are precisely
the equilibrium and welfare objects audited by the
independent Stage-4A programs.

\subsection{Uniform interval proof on the entire compatibility domain}

The canonical Stage-4A certificate covers the policy continuum
as the union of 1,000 exact adjacent closed rational intervals
$G_k=[k/1000,(k+1)/1000]$ for $k=0,\ldots,999$.
For each cell, a numerical candidate merely supplies the centre
of an outward-rounded rational design rectangle $Z_k$.
For SU the parameter-uniform Krawczyk operator is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app_krawczyk}
 {\cal K}(z_0,Z_k,G_k)
 =z_0-CF(z_0,G_k)
 +\left[I-C D_{(u,v)}F(Z_k,G_k)\right](Z_k-z_0),
\end{equation}
where $F=(F_M,F_O)$ and $C$ is a numerical preconditioner.
Each $Z_k$ is centered at a numerical root rounded to
12 decimal places and converted to an exact rational;
its two coordinate half-widths are $1/5000$.
The numeric inverse Jacobian preconditioner $C$ is
similarly rounded to 12 decimals and rationalized.
All inclusion comparisons use outward interval arithmetic
with 35 decimal digits. The numerical approximation
is permitted only because the final interval operator
strictly encloses its effect. The algorithm checks
the rational rectangles lie in the interior design cube
and evaluates $\mathcal K(Z_k,G_k)\subset
\operatorname{int}(Z_k)$ with a nonsingular Jacobian.
For the international scalar root in $Y_k$,
the interval Newton test is
$N=y_0-F_I(y_0,G_k)/F_{I,y}(Y_k,G_k)
\subset\operatorname{int}(Y_k)$ with $F_{I,y}<0$.
At this witness all 1,000 rational policy cells pass
without adaptive subdivision. These box tests
certify roots for every $g$ in their closed cells;
Lemma~\ref{lem:design} supplies global firm Nash
uniqueness on the full asymmetric cube.

For each enclosed continuation the computer evaluates
the derivatives defined by \eqref{eq:member-path} and
\eqref{eq:outsider-path}, including the implicit design-response
terms. Strict negative outward upper bounds throughout the
entire collection of policy cells give
\eqref{eq:signed-bounds}. Combined with equality of all
regimes at $g=0$ they imply the strict inequalities for
every $g>0$; a numerical policy grid would not imply that result.

For the frozen counterfactual, the true SW root is itself
enclosed by rational FOC sign changes. The program holds
that root fixed across histories, verifies the strict
C and member consent margins at $g=.05$, and certifies
the \emph{supremum} of member SU welfare over 500 closed
rational $g$ intervals. This establishes
\eqref{eq:frozen-envelope} over the entire offer set.

\subsection{The separate nonlinear experiment}

Under \eqref{eq:quadratic-map}, replace the definitions of
$h,n,t$ with
\[
 h^{(2)}=(\rho-\eta)g+\beta u^2,\quad
 n^{(2)}=\beta(u^2+v^2)/2,\quad
 t^{(2)}=(\rho-\eta)g+\beta y^2.
\]
Because each off-diagonal entry responds to design
with marginal effect $\beta x_i$, its IS design FOC becomes
\[
 F_I^{(2)}(y,g)=\kappa(1-y)-
 \frac{12\beta y\alpha}
 {(\alpha-t^{(2)})(\alpha+2t^{(2)})^3}=0.
\]
The repaired nonlinear certificate uses a SymPy
rational-coefficient printer that constructs every
coefficient as an outward interval $\operatorname{iv}(p)/q$,
rather than evaluating $p/q$ as binary64 before
interval evaluation. In the global pseudo-gradient
Jacobian, Gershgorin rows use interval sums of
the diagonal and absolute symmetrized off-diagonals;
only the strict negative interval upper bound
determines acceptance. At the exact witness the
global three-firm Rosen check covers the full
four-dimensional $(x_A,x_B,x_C,g)$ cube with
one $\SW$ box, one $\SU$ box and five $\IS$
boxes; the least favorable reported row upper
bound is below $-0.0949634$.

The equilibrium-root face checks use the closed SU
rectangle $[0.60,0.67]\times[0.60,0.65]$ and
the closed IS interval $[0.60,0.73]$.
SU face signs are checked over 40 rational
counterpart subintervals and 100 rational
policy cells, with positive inward FOCs on
the low faces and negative FOCs on the high faces;
the same sign test covers both IS faces.
Poincare--Miranda gives an interior root in
each SU rectangle for every policy depth;
the intermediate-value theorem gives the IS
root. The global Rosen result makes those
interior roots the unique Nash equilibria.

For each of 100 adjacent closed depth cells
the certificate then encloses the design root
in local rational rectangles of half-width
$1/400$ about numerical proposals that are
rounded and converted to exact rationals.
All six SU/IS faces retain strict inward signs
on each policy cell; the 2-by-2 implicit
derivative determinant and IS scalar derivative
exclude zero. The interval total member
welfare derivative and accession-margin derivative
have upper bounds below $-0.01427873$ and
$-0.01657547$, respectively, covering the
whole closed $g\in[0,1]$ continuum.
The frozen SU policy upper envelope uses 4,000
closed rational depth cells. The originally
pre-registered frozen C rejection at $g=.05$
remains an adverse result. No baseline root,
linearity-specific Rosen inequality or binary64
coefficient is substituted into this variant.
